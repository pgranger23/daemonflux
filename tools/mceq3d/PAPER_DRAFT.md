## Abstract

We extend the muon-calibrated, one-dimensional `daemonflux` model into a fully directional, absolute, all-flavour atmospheric-lepton flux $\Phi(E,\cos\theta_z,\varphi)$ over $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$, the range that drives atmospheric-neutrino oscillation measurements. The flux is a deterministic product of a per-zenith curved-atmosphere cascade, a solar-modulation factor, and a **single joint integral** over the production altitude *and* the primary-direction cone which carries both the off-axis 3D production geometry and the cascade-correct full-IGRF geomagnetic cutoff — replacing the product of two separately cone-averaged factors and restoring the production–cutoff covariance it dropped. The cone is channel-resolved — direct pion, kaon and muon-decay components, each with its own generator-derived width and weighted by MCEq's own depth-resolved production — so the off-axis 3D-production factor $E_{\rm off}$ is **species-dependent** (horizon/vertical $\approx 1.9$ for $\nu_\mu$ and $\approx 2.3$ for $\nu_e$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$), with no hand-set hadronic parameters and no reference-flux input. The rigidity cutoff is a $\le 1\,\mathrm{GV}$ ladder plus bisection to $0.1\,\mathrm{GV}$ on a limb-refined sky map, and the measured penumbra (a $0.1\,\mathrm{GV}$ back-trace ladder finds *zero* sub-cutoff bandwidth in every high-cutoff direction) makes the transmission an exact bin-averaged step. With a Global-Spline-Fit-anchored hybrid base the model reproduces the Honda HKKM2014 solar-minimum tables to $0.90$–$1.10$ for **all four** charge/flavour species over Honda's entire $10\times12\times101$ grid (median $|\log_{10}$ ratio$|$ $\approx 5$–$9\%$, $90$th percentile $\approx 15$–$23\%$), with the flavour ratio within $8\%$, both $\nu/\bar\nu$ charge ratios within $\approx 4\%$ at the vertical and $8\%$ at the horizon, and the Honda-independent Bartol calculation confirming the vertical flux to $0.88$–$1.02$. The sub-GeV near-horizon excess — the largest genuinely-3D feature — is reproduced from first principles: horizon/vertical lands within $5$–$12\%$ of Honda for every species over $0.3$–$3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, comparable to the $8$–$15\%$ Honda–Bartol mutual spread. The charge-dependent East–West effect, which requires a shift-sensitive observable (the first azimuthal harmonic) and Honda's own azimuth convention to measure at all, is present with the correct sign and species assignment: the model carries $60$–$145\%$ of Honda's $\nu$-versus-$\bar\nu$ **phase** splitting at $75$–$87^\circ$, but only $15$–$25\%$ of its **amplitude** splitting, and the four-species ordering of the West/East amplitude that Honda shows near the horizon is the principal remaining limitation. Two further limitations are quantified rather than tuned away: a $6$–$13\%$ horizon/vertical overshoot against Bartol at $0.3$–$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ accompanied by a genuine $\approx 5\%$ net solid-angle excess in the production factor, and a $\nu_\mu$ East–West overshoot of $+19$/$+24\%$ at $87^\circ$ and $1$/$2\,\mathrm{GeV}$. A third limitation reported in an earlier draft — a species-independent $\approx 13^\circ$ rotation of the azimuthal dipole relative to Honda — is now closed: anchoring the geomagnetic cutoff of every cone sample at the neutrino's own production point rather than at the detector (a cached, one-time-per-site displaced-site map family) removes essentially the whole effect with no free parameter, taking the North/South model-over-Honda mismatch from $0.86$–$1.23$ to $0.91$–$1.05$ and the dipole-phase offset from $12$–$14^\circ$ to $\le 1.3^\circ$ at every species. All systematics are delivered as named nuisance pulls on `daemonflux`'s calibration covariance: an NA61-driven $\pm 8\%$ hadronic term on the horizon excess, solar modulation, and the sub-GeV base-model spread ($\pm 14\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$) — a correlated error model the references lack. The cached geomagnetic cutoff makes the flux fast enough for routine oscillation fits.

## 1. Introduction

Atmospheric neutrinos are a cornerstone source for oscillation physics, from the original discovery of oscillations [1] to current and next-generation measurements of the mass ordering and mixing parameters at Super-Kamiokande, IceCube/DeepCore, Hyper-Kamiokande, DUNE, JUNO and KM3NeT/ORCA [1–5]. The interpretation of these data rests on a prediction of the atmospheric-neutrino flux as a function of energy and arrival direction. Below a few GeV — the region that drives the sub-dominant oscillation signatures — the flux prediction must be genuinely three-dimensional and must include geomagnetic effects, and the residual flux uncertainty in this region is among the leading systematics of the measurements.

State-of-the-art flux calculations (Honda *et al.* / HKKM [6,23], Bartol [7], FLUKA [8]) are full three-dimensional Monte-Carlo cascade simulations through the geomagnetic field and a realistic atmosphere. They are authoritative but computationally heavy, are tabulated for a fixed set of sites, and carry the hadronic-model uncertainties of their underlying interaction codes. A complementary approach, `daemonflux` [10], parametrises MCEq [11] cascade calculations with splines and calibrates them against a global set of muon measurements, yielding a fast, data-anchored flux *with a rigorous nuisance-parameter covariance*. By construction, however, `daemonflux` is one-dimensional: the neutrino is taken collinear with the primary, and the flux is azimuthally symmetric and free of any geomagnetic cutoff.

This work bridges the two: it retains the calibrated normalisation and uncertainty model of the 1D approach while adding the directional and geomagnetic structure of the 3D calculations, in a fast, deterministic engine that can be evaluated at an arbitrary site. We deliberately separate the physics into a factorised, trusted-ingredient construction (Section 3), validate each ingredient against data or against the authoritative 3D tables (Section 4), and quantify the irreducible low-energy uncertainty as an explicit systematic (Section 5). Section 6 discusses scope and limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2. The one-dimensional baseline

The starting point is the inclusive 1D flux $\Phi^{1D}_s(E,\theta_z)$ for each neutrino species $s \in \{\nu_\mu,\bar\nu_\mu,\nu_e,\bar\nu_e\}$. We support two choices. The first is a direct MCEq [11] solution with the SIBYLL-2.3d hadronic interaction model [12] and the Hillas–Gaisser H3a primary-flux parametrisation [14], solved per zenith in the realistic CORSIKA US-Standard layered atmosphere. The second, and our recommended choice for the absolute scale, is the muon-calibrated `daemonflux` flux [10]: it provides the $E^3$-weighted species sums and the $\nu/\bar\nu$ ratios, from which we reconstruct the four species (Section 3.2). The MCEq base is fully self-contained but carries the SIBYLL/H3a normalisation, which we find lies $\approx 25$–$30\%$ below Honda in the sub-GeV–few-GeV band (Section 4.1); the `daemonflux` base removes this deficit by anchoring to muon data.

**Outputs, conventions and uncertainties.** The engine returns the absolute differential flux $\Phi_s$ for the four species in $\mathrm{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}\,GeV^{-1}}$ on a logarithmic energy grid ($\approx 10$ bins per decade) spanning $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$, evaluable at arbitrary $(E,\cos\theta_z,\varphi)$ by trilinear interpolation (log-$E$, azimuth-periodic, $\cos\theta_z$). The same 1D base also carries the inclusive **muon** flux and charge ratio (charge-separated $\mu^\pm$), so a directional muon flux follows by the identical construction; we do not pursue it here. On uncertainties, two scopes are distinguished: (i) the **model/normalisation** spread between the two bases, delivered as an explicit systematic (Section 5); and (ii) daemonflux's own **nuisance-parameter (muon-calibration) covariance**, its principal feature, which **is propagated** to the directional flux (`solve(with_calib_error=True)`, Section 5). Because $G$ and $S$ are independent of the daemonflux parameters, the fractional calibration error is preserved exactly, $\sigma(\Phi_{3D})/\Phi_{3D}=\sigma(\Phi_{\rm df})/\Phi_{\rm df}$ (verified to $<0.1\%$).

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Deterministic-3D construction

The absolute directional flux is built from the per-zenith curved-atmosphere cascade, redistributed in arrival direction by the production geometry and modulated by the geomagnetic cutoff,

$$
\Phi_s(E,\cos\theta_z,\varphi) \;=\; \Phi^{1D}_s\!\left(E,\,|\cos\theta_z|\right)\; F_s\!\left(E,\cos\theta_z,\varphi\right)\; S(E),
\qquad
F_s \;\equiv\; \frac{J_s}{p_{\rm axis}} ,
\tag{1}
$$

where $\Phi^{1D}_s$ is the per-zenith 1D cascade (carrying the curved-atmosphere sec θ, Section 2), $S(E)$ is the optional solar force-field factor (Section 5), and $F_s$ is a **single integral over the production altitude and the primary-direction cone** of the depth-resolved production $p(X_{\rm slant},E)$ times the cascade-correct geomagnetic response $G_s(E,R_c)$, normalised by the collinear production $p_{\rm axis}$ (Section 3.6, Eq. 8b). $F_s$ therefore carries in one object what earlier versions of this work factorised into the off-axis 3D-production factor $E_{\rm off}=\Phi_{3D}/\Phi_{1D}$ (Section 3.6) and the cutoff response $G_s\in[0,1]$ (Section 3.4),

$$
F_s \;\longrightarrow\; E_{\rm off}(E,\cos\theta_z)\;G_s\!\left(E,R_c(\cos\theta_z,\varphi)\right) \quad\text{(factorised limit)},
\tag{1b}
$$

which the engine still uses for up-going directions and for energies outside the tabulated $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ range; setting $G\equiv 1$ in $F_s$ returns the tabulated $E_{\rm off}$ exactly (verified to $6\times10^{-15}$, `test_mceq3d_flux.py`), so the two paths are the same physics. The product form drops the covariance between production and cutoff across the cone — zero at the vertical, largest at the horizon — which is why the joint integral, not the product, is delivered (Section 3.6). Each ingredient is a *first-principles or validated* piece: $\Phi^{1D}$ is muon-calibrated, $p(X,E)$ and the cone widths come from MCEq production and generator kinematics, $G$ is the first-principles rigidity response. The construction is genuinely 3D — every mechanism (curved geometry, off-axis production, geomagnetic anisotropy, charge-signed muon bending) enters — while remaining a fast deterministic integral rather than a Monte-Carlo. The absolute value $|\cos\theta_z|$ enters the base because atmospheric production is up/down symmetric; the up/down asymmetry of the *observed* flux arises through $R_c$ (Section 3.5). $F_s\to 1$ at high energy, recovering the calibrated 1D flux. (Setting $E_{\rm off}=1$ with `solve(offaxis=False)` gives the fast geomagnetic-only path; without the off-axis geometry the $\nu_\mu$ horizon/vertical ratio at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ is a factor $1.86$ smaller.)

### 3.2 Species reconstruction from the data-anchored base

`daemonflux` reports the $E^3$-weighted flux *sums* and the species ratios. Writing $\Sigma_\mu \equiv E^3(\Phi_{\nu_\mu}+\Phi_{\bar\nu_\mu})$ and $r_\mu \equiv \Phi_{\nu_\mu}/\Phi_{\bar\nu_\mu}$ (and analogously for $e$), the individual species follow from

$$
\Phi_{\nu_\mu} = \frac{\Sigma_\mu}{E^3}\,\frac{r_\mu}{1+r_\mu},\qquad
\Phi_{\bar\nu_\mu} = \frac{\Sigma_\mu}{E^3}\,\frac{1}{1+r_\mu},
\tag{2}
$$

with the cm$^{-2}\to$m$^{-2}$ unit conversion applied. The reconstruction is valid over the calibrated range; below $\sim 0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the splines extrapolate beyond the muon data, an effect we treat as a systematic (Section 5).

### 3.3 Geomagnetic cutoff by trajectory back-tracing

The directional rigidity cutoff $R_c(\cos\theta_z,\varphi)$ is computed from first principles rather than from the analytic Størmer formula [17]. A cosmic ray of rigidity $R = pc/Ze$ moving with unit tangent $\mathbf{u}=d\mathbf{r}/ds$ obeys

$$
\frac{d\mathbf{u}}{ds} = \frac{c}{R}\,\big(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})\big),
\tag{3}
$$

i.e. a gyration of radius $r_g = R/(cB_\perp)$. To test whether a positive primary of rigidity $R$ can reach the detector from a direction $\mathbf{d}$, we integrate the *reversed* trajectory (reversed velocity and charge) outward from the detector with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta scheme in arc length: if it escapes to large radius the rigidity is **allowed**; if it returns to the Earth it is **forbidden** [16]. The field $\mathbf{B}$ is the full IGRF-13 model [15] (degree 13) near the Earth, smoothly matched to the tilted-dipole term beyond a few Earth radii where the higher multipoles are negligible.

**Locating the cutoff.** $R_c$ is the *upper* cutoff $R_U$: the highest forbidden rigidity met when scanning downward from the ceiling. A single linear ladder is not a robust way to find it, because the topmost forbidden band can be narrower than the step and the penumbra contains narrow allowed islands: a $24$-point ladder over $0.5$–$55\,\mathrm{GV}$ ($2.37\,\mathrm{GV}$ step) samples $9.98\,\mathrm{GV}$ at the Kamioka vertical, which lies inside the allowed island $[9.63,10.13]$, steps straight over the forbidden band ending at $\approx 11.5\,\mathrm{GV}$, and returns $8.79\,\mathrm{GV}$ — a $24\%$ error, on $37\%$ of the down-going cells. The delivered scan (`geomag_backtrace.scan_upper_cutoff`) therefore walks a coarse ladder whose step is $\le 1\,\mathrm{GV}$ — below the width of the topmost forbidden band everywhere on the Kamioka sky — and then **bisects** the bracket [last allowed, first forbidden] to $0.1\,\mathrm{GV}$, at a cost of $\approx 4$ extra traces per direction rather than the $\sim\!550$ a $0.1\,\mathrm{GV}$ ladder would need. The ceiling is $r_{\rm hi}=55\,\mathrm{GV}$, above the extreme-horizon East cutoff, and saturated cells are flagged rather than silently clamped. Direct back-traces at Kamioka (2020-01-01) give a vertical cutoff of $11.37\,\mathrm{GV}$, consistent with the literature value ($\approx 11.3\,\mathrm{GV}$), $41.72\,\mathrm{GV}$ at $87^\circ$ East and $7.47\,\mathrm{GV}$ at $87^\circ$ West. As a closure test, replacing the field by a centred, aligned dipole reproduces the Størmer cutoff: $14.9\,\mathrm{GV}$ vertical at the geomagnetic equator, the $\cos^4(\lambda)$ latitude dependence, and the correct sign of the East–West asymmetry (positive primaries arrive preferentially from the West).

**Interpolation and the limb.** Because back-tracing each direction is expensive, the $(\theta_z,\varphi)$ cutoff map is precomputed once per site and cached; subsequent evaluations are bilinear interpolations. On a uniform $7.4^\circ$ zenith grid that interpolation over-estimates the East cutoff between nodes, where it rises convexly, by $+1.7$ to $+3.3\,\mathrm{GV}$ at $84$–$88^\circ$ — comparable to the whole effect being measured. The delivered map therefore carries dense nodes at the limb ($82$, $84$, $85.5$, $87$, $88$, $88.5$, $89$, $89.5^\circ$; $42$ zenith nodes in total, $8$ of them within $10^\circ$ of the horizon), which reduces the interpolation error at $84$–$88^\circ$ East to $\le 0.06\,\mathrm{GV}$. The Kamioka map spans $0.50$–$52.18\,\mathrm{GV}$ with no saturated cells; a cold full-sphere build is $60$ traces per direction, $\approx 2500\,\mathrm{s}$ for the down-going hemisphere and $\approx 6200\,\mathrm{s}$ for the up-going one (run concurrently), after which every evaluation is a table lookup.

Near the horizon a substantial part of the production cone (Section 3.6) points **below** the local horizon at the detector: at $87^\circ$, $42$/$37$/$27\%$ of the cone solid-angle weight at $0.3$/$0.5$/$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Earlier versions of this work read the antipodal far-side cutoff for those samples, stitching the down-going map onto the up-going one and putting a $9.5\,\mathrm{GV}$ discontinuity across $1^\circ$ at $89^\circ$ into the delivered map — exactly where the charge-signed muon-bending shift (Section 3.7) has to be evaluated. The delivered treatment instead transforms every down-going cone sample into the **production-point frame** ($h_{\rm prod}=30\,\mathrm{km}$ up the arrival ray, which is the dominant part of the production-point displacement: the local zenith of an $89^\circ$ arrival direction is $84.3^\circ$ there, worth $\approx 7\,\mathrm{GV}$) and reads it off the down-going map only; samples still below the horizon at $P$ are Earth-shadowed and are dropped from the numerator *and* the normalisation ($p\to0$), never assigned a far-side cutoff. The delivered down-going path therefore never touches the far-side map and has no seam. Since 2026-09-05 the residual position/altitude term is also removed: `cutoff_anchor="prod_point"` (now the default) additionally launches the back-trace from the displaced point itself, rather than only rotating the frame. The analytic Størmer cutoff remains available as a dependency-free fallback.

**The production-point-anchored cutoff.** Because back-tracing every displaced site from scratch would be prohibitive, we exploit the very different smoothness of the cutoff's two dependencies: it is sharp in *direction* ($\approx1.5\,\mathrm{GV/deg}$ in the East above $84^\circ$) but smooth in *site*, through the geomagnetic latitude ($R_c\sim\cos^4\lambda_{\rm mag}$, $d\ln R_c/d\lambda\approx-0.034/\mathrm{deg}$ at Kamioka). We therefore precompute, once per site and date, a $3\times3$ Cartesian **family** of full down-going cutoff maps at ground offsets $(d_{\rm north},d_{\rm east})\in\{-D,0,+D\}^2$ with $D=617\,\mathrm{km}$ (the displacement at the exact horizon for $h_{\rm prod}=30\,\mathrm{km}$) — the centre node is, by construction, the ordinary detector map — and interpolate it bilinearly in the site offset only; the direction (zenith/azimuth) axes always keep the family's own fine limb-refined nodes. Every cone sample is then read in the production point's *exact* local frame, including the convergence of the meridians ($\lesssim4^\circ$ of azimuth for an east-west displacement), which the frame rotation above omits. The family costs $4200$ extra back-traced cutoffs; a seeded band scan — each displaced-site direction's search window is seeded by the same direction's already-known detector cutoff, exploiting that the two differ by no more than the $\lesssim20\%$ effect being modelled — makes this $\approx4\times$ cheaper than a naive scan, for a measured one-time cost of $4.1\,\mathrm{h}$ on $46$ cores, cached alongside the detector map. Validated against direct back-traces launched at the production point, the family agrees to $0.16$–$0.51\,\mathrm{GV}$ at the cardinal directions and reproduces the North/South ratio and dipole phase to $4$–$7\%$ (Section 6); the vertical is an exact no-op. The one open approximation is the bilinear site interpolation itself, which has measured $8$–$22\%$ outliers in the low-cutoff West/South-West cells (median cell $\approx1\%$); a finer $5\times5$ family would quarter them at $\approx4\times$ the build cost. The fast, $\approx1$–$2\,\mathrm{GV}$-approximate `cutoff_anchor="detector"` path (rotate the frame only, as above) remains available for rapid A/B studies.

### 3.4 Cascade-correct suppression and per-nucleus rigidity

The geomagnetic cut acts on the primary nucleons inside the developing shower, not on the neutrino energy. We therefore define the suppression factor as the ratio of two full cascade solutions,

$$
G_s(E,R_c) \;=\; \frac{\Phi_s\big[\text{primaries cut at } R_c\big]}{\Phi_s\big[\text{all primaries}\big]},
\tag{4}
$$

which automatically folds in the spread of primary energies feeding a given neutrino energy and avoids the common approximation of applying an effective-rigidity factor directly to the secondary spectrum.

The cut is on rigidity, $R = (A/Z)\,E_{\rm nucleon}$, so free protons ($A/Z=1$) and nucleons bound in heavier nuclei ($A/Z\simeq 2$) are removed at different nucleon energies. We split the primary nucleon flux accordingly, using MCEq's own proton and neutron spectra: the free-proton fraction is the proton–neutron excess,

$$
f_{\rm free}(E) = \frac{\Phi_p(E)-\Phi_n(E)}{\Phi_p(E)},
\tag{5}
$$

and the bound nucleons carry a composition-weighted $\langle A/Z\rangle$ from the H3a abundances ($\approx 2.005$, rising slowly with energy as the iron fraction grows). The per-nucleon transmission combines the two channels,

$$
T(E;R_c) = f_{\rm free}\,T_1(E;R_c) + (1-f_{\rm free})\,T_{\langle A/Z\rangle}(E;R_c),
\tag{6}
$$

with a rigidity step of width $\sigma$ in $\ln R$, **bin-averaged** over the (log-uniform) primary energy bin,

$$
T_a(E;R_c) = \Big\langle\, \tfrac12\left[1 + \operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{\ln(aE')-\ln R_c}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)\right] \Big\rangle_{\ln E' \in [\ln E \pm \Delta/2]},\qquad a \equiv A/Z .
\tag{7}
$$

Neglecting the free/bound split over-suppresses the bound component near the sub-GeV horizon. The A/Z split is unaffected by everything below: the back-traced trajectory depends on the primary only through its rigidity, so at equal $R$ every fully stripped species follows the identical path, and $A/Z$ enters only through $E\to R$, which is exactly how Eq. (7) uses it — one width, two $a$.

**The penumbra width is measured, and it is zero.** For a *fixed* direction the back-traced admittance is binary, so what the erf has to represent is the penumbral band $[R_L,R_U]$ in which allowed and forbidden rigidities alternate. A $0.1\,\mathrm{GV}$ ladder at Kamioka (2020-01-01, $\sim\!1000$ trajectories per direction; `diag_penumbra_width.py`) measures that band directly:

In every East / high-cutoff direction — the ones that make the East–West asymmetry — $R_L > R_U$: the lowest allowed rigidity lies *above* the highest forbidden one, i.e. the transition is a sharp step with no penumbral structure at all. Only the low-cutoff directions show any band, and there it is a couple of narrow allowed islands, a small downward *shift* of the effective cutoff rather than a width. The delivered value is therefore $\sigma_{\ln R}=0$ (`SIGMA_LNR`), the exact step. The bin average of Eq. (7) is what makes that usable: MCEq holds bin-averaged primary fluxes, so the exact representation of a cut falling inside a bin is that bin's overlap fraction, and taking it analytically keeps $G_s(R_c)$ **continuous** in $R_c$ where point-sampling a hard step would make it a staircase moving only when $R_c$ crosses a bin edge ($\Delta\ln R=\Delta\ln E\approx0.115$, i.e. $\approx4.6\,\mathrm{GV}$ at $40\,\mathrm{GV}$) — destroying exactly the near-limb response the East–West asymmetry is made of. The residual grid ramp has an RMS of $0.033$, itself at the level of the $87^\circ$ S / $87^\circ$ W measurements, so nothing narrower is representable and nothing wider is supported by the tracer.

The legacy value was $\sigma=0.5$ — hand-set, inherited from the analytic model in the `daemonflux` package, never derived, and far too wide: it passes $8\%$ of the primaries at $R_c/2$, opening $6.3\,\mathrm{GV}$ of allowed rigidity below the $41.6\,\mathrm{GV}$ East cutoff where the back-tracer measures none. Against the steep primary spectrum that tail carried $\approx 40\%$ of the surviving East flux at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Sharpening it lowers $G$ at the East cutoff by $12.5\%$ ($0.1889\to0.1653$ for $\nu_\mu$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$) while moving the $11.5\,\mathrm{GV}$ vertical by $0.3\%$; every species' West/East amplitude rises by a nearly uniform $12$–$15$ points. A scan over $\sigma\in\{0.5,0.25,0.1,0.03,0\}$ shows every delivered observable saturating below $\sigma\approx0.1$ (the $0.1$, $0.03$ and $0$ columns differ by $<0.5\%$), so the whole measured non-vertical range maps onto the same answer and a single width is adequate; at $\sigma=0.5$ the bin average itself shifts $T$ by $\le6\times10^{-4}$, so this change is a change of physics input, not of numerics.

A potential concern is that $G_s$ is precomputed on the vertical column, whereas at large zenith the slant atmosphere is much thicker and the shower develops differently (more complete meson decay, more muon energy loss). We tested this directly by recomputing Eq. (4) in MCEq from vertical to $\cos\theta_z = 0.1$ ($\approx 84^\circ$): $G_s$ is zenith-independent to $\le 2.1\%$ at the worst point ($0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $R_c=11\,\mathrm{GV}$) and to $\le 0.4\%$ above $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Figure 2). The slant-depth effects act on numerator and denominator of Eq. (4) alike and largely cancel. $G_s$ is nonetheless recomputed per zenith in the delivered configuration, so this residual is removed from the shipped flux.

### 3.5 The up-going hemisphere

For up-going directions the neutrino is produced on the far side of the Earth and traverses the planet essentially undeflected, so atmospheric production is identical to the down-going case at $|\cos\theta_z|$ (hence the absolute value in Eq. 1), but the geomagnetic cutoff must be evaluated at the **far-side production point**, not at the detector. Given the detector position and arrival direction we compute the line-of-sight intersection with the production shell at altitude $h\simeq 20\,\mathrm{km}$ on the opposite limb, and evaluate $R_c$ there with the primary direction set equal to the (straight-line) neutrino direction. This makes the up-going flux a genuine global-geomagnetic prediction. The leading approximation is the use of a single representative production point per direction; the finite extent of the production region is a sub-leading geometric correction.

### 3.6 The off-axis 3D-production factor $E_{\rm off}$

The genuinely-3D effect that a per-zenith cascade cannot produce is the *off-axis production geometry*: a neutrino arriving from zenith $\theta_z$ was created at a point $P$ at altitude $h$ along the arrival ray, by a shower whose axis (the primary direction) need not be the arrival direction — it lies within the production cone of the parent chain. In a *curved* atmosphere the local zenith of a direction steepens with altitude along a near-horizontal ray, so the slant depth $X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi)$ traversed by the parent — and hence the local neutrino production per slant depth $p(X,E)=d\Phi_\nu/dX$ — varies strongly across that cone near the horizon. Averaging the production over the cone therefore differs from the 1D (collinear) value:

$$
E_{\rm off}(\theta_z,E) = \frac{\displaystyle\int d\ell\,\rho(h)\,\big\langle\, p\!\left(X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi_p),E\right)\big\rangle_{\rm cone}}{\displaystyle\int d\ell\,\rho(h)\; p\!\left(X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi_o),E\right)},
\tag{8}
$$

with $\psi_o$ the arrival-direction local zenith at $P$ and $\psi_p$ the primary local zenith for a cone offset. Near the horizon ($\psi_o\!\to\!90^\circ$) the cone reaches primaries that arrived much more vertically — shallower $X_{\rm slant}$, younger shower, more sub-GeV production — while its opposite half points below the local horizon (Earth-shadowed, $X_{\rm slant}=\infty$, $p\!\to\!0$); the asymmetry enhances the horizon at the expense of the vertical.

**The delivered object is the joint integral, not the product.** The geomagnetic cutoff varies across the same cone that the production varies across, and it is *correlated* with it: in the East a more vertical primary has both a smaller $X_{\rm slant}$ (higher $p$) and a lower $R_c$ (higher $G$), while in the West the cutoff gradient flips sign. The product $\langle p\rangle_{\rm cone}\langle G\rangle_{\rm cone}$ drops that covariance — exactly zero at the vertical, largest at the horizon. The delivered engine evaluates instead the single integral

$$
J_s(E,\mathbf{n}) = \int d\ell\,\rho(h)\int d\Omega_p\,W(\alpha)\; p_s\!\left(X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi_p),E\right)\,G_s\!\left(E,R_c(\mathbf{n}_p,\mathbf{r})\right),
\qquad F_s = \frac{J_s}{p_{\rm axis}},
\tag{8b}
$$

(`joint_cone.delivered_joint_factor`), with $R_c$ read in the production-point frame (Section 3.3) and the Earth shadow enforced by the production profile itself, so a blocked primary drops out of the numerator *and* of the normalisation. This matters because at $87^\circ$ the sub-limb part of the cone is $42$/$37$/$27\%$ of the solid-angle weight at $0.3$/$0.5$/$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ but carries only $4$–$7\%$ of the production: in the product form those samples contributed a far-side $G$ they have no production to weight. Setting $G\equiv1$ in Eq. (8b) returns Eq. (8) exactly, so the two halves of the engine share one cone definition, one moment set and one channel decomposition.

**The cone is channel-resolved, so $E_{\rm off}$ is species-dependent.** The depth-resolved production is split into its parent channels using MCEq's own tracked categories, $p_s = p_s^{\rm dir} + p_s^{K} + p_s^{\mu}$, and each is folded with its own width: the direct pion cone $\sigma_\pi$, the kaon cone $\sigma_K$, and a muon-decay cone $\sigma_{\mu,s}$ computed per flavour by exact Monte-Carlo (`kinematic_kernel.mudecay_shape_mc`) — the generator meson angle, exact two-body $\pi\to\mu\nu$ kinematics with the muon energy *sampled* rather than fixed at $E_\mu=3E_\nu$, and the exact Michel boost of a massless daughter. Because Eq. (8) is linear in $p$, summing per-channel numerators over the common denominator *is* the production-weighted blend with weights $f_c=D_c/\sum D$, which are then depth-resolved (the muon-decay fraction rises toward the horizon by itself) and species-resolved, with no separate zenith-independent channel-fraction table. This makes the whole factor genuinely different per species: at $87^\circ$ and $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the delivered $E_{\rm off}$ is $1.699$/$1.721$/$1.767$/$1.769$ for $\nu_\mu$/$\bar\nu_\mu$/$\nu_e$/$\bar\nu_e$.

*This retracts a claim of an earlier version of this work.* That version stated that "the neutrino's own decay-opening angle does not enter the cone, since it relocates individual neutrinos but not the pion-production excess", and applied one flavour-blind pion cone to every channel. That is wrong: the cone in Eq. (8) is the distribution of the *primary* direction given the *neutrino* direction, so the whole decay chain contributes to it in quadrature (Lipari [9]), and a $\nu_e$ — $98\%$ of which come from muon decay — samples a visibly wider cone than a directly produced $\nu_\mu$. The corrected muon-decay cone is $34.6^\circ$ at $0.2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ where the previous surrogate gave $26.5^\circ$.

**The cone widths are generator moments, with a corrected angle definition.** Consistent with the MCEq/`daemonflux` philosophy, the widths come from the *same generator* that provides the yields rather than from a parametrisation: the inclusive $\langle\theta^2\rangle(E_{\rm meson})$ measured from SIBYLL-2.3d / UrQMD-3.4 through `chromo`, folded with exact decay kinematics and weighted by the primary spectrum. There are **no hand-set transverse-momentum or Feynman-$x$ parameters**. The moment extraction used $\theta=\arctan(p_T/p)$ with $p$ the *total* momentum; the correct relation is $\sin\theta = p_T/p$, and the pipeline now has one canonical definition (`kernel_regeneration.production_angle`). Regenerating the moments on the same UrQMD-3.4/SIBYLL-2.3d recipe makes $\sqrt{\langle\theta^2\rangle}$ for $\pi^+$ $39\%$ larger at a secondary energy of $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ ($28.9^\circ\to40.1^\circ$), $32\%$ at $0.5$, $14\%$ at $1$, $5\%$ at $2$ and $0.3\%$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — a same-sample comparison free of Monte-Carlo scatter gives $1.456$/$1.324$/$1.131$/$1.046$/$1.003$ over the same points. Propagated to the cone that drives the excess, $\sigma_\pi$ widens from $18.72^\circ$ to $20.85^\circ$ at $E_\nu=0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ ($+11\%$) and from $6.48^\circ$ to $6.90^\circ$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$; $\sigma_K$ from $48.68^\circ$ to $56.30^\circ$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The correction is validated against data in Section 4.2: it halves the UrQMD-versus-NA61 discrepancy in $\langle\theta\rangle(p_{\rm lab})$ below $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, from $7.5\%$ low to $3.4\%$ low, for both pion charges.

**Flux conservation.** Eq. (8) is not a heuristic redistribution kernel: it *is* the exact straight-line 3D transport of an isotropic primary flux through a curved atmosphere. Writing out the emissivity from first principles — the volume element is independent of the primary direction, so no Jacobian enters, and the decay kernel $K(\alpha)$ is azimuthally symmetric about the primary axis, so conditioning on the neutrino direction rather than the primary direction introduces no reciprocity factor — the line integral of that emissivity along the arrival ray reproduces Eq. (8)'s numerator exactly. This is not merely asserted: in a *flat* atmosphere the exact expression has the closed form $F_{\rm flat}(\theta_z)=\langle\cos\alpha\rangle_K$ at the vertical (independent of $\theta_z$ wherever the cone stays clear of the local horizon), and the implementation reproduces this to $0.02$-$0.12\%$ per channel (`diag_conservation.py --flat`, `test_conservation.py`).

What this construction conserves is *not* the down-going solid-angle average $\langle F\rangle_\Omega$ quoted in earlier drafts of this work. Neutrinos crossing the ground are counted with the measure $\int_{\rm down}d\Omega\,\cos\psi\,\Phi$, i.e. the ground-crossing average $\langle F\rangle_{\cos}$. Evaluated in the exact kernel's flat-atmosphere closed form — where flux conservation is provable — the *plain* solid-angle average $\langle F\rangle_\Omega$ is **not** $1$: it is $1.21$/$1.15$/$1.10$/$1.04$ at $0.2$/$0.3$/$0.5$/$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, *larger* than the curved delivered value of $1.065$/$1.046$/$1.024$/$1.008$, because $F\sim1/\cos\psi_o$ at the horizon and $\langle\cdot\rangle_\Omega$ weights the horizon exactly as it weights the vertical; the number is measure-dependent, growing without bound as the horizon bin is refined further. In the ground-crossing measure, by contrast, the delivered channel-resolved factor is a **$5.5\%$ deficit** at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — $4.4\%$ already present in the flat-atmosphere limit (large-$\alpha$ emission that a flat slab loses upward through the local horizon) and $1.1\%$ genuine sphericity (near-horizontal neutrinos skimming over the curved limb and escaping). **The construction manufactures no flux; in the physically conserved measure it removes roughly $5\%$.**

The $\approx5\%$ number reported as a systematic in earlier drafts of this work is therefore a property of the measure used to quote it, not evidence that the construction over-produces flux. The real, quantified uncertainty on the sub-GeV zenith shape is the width of the production-and-decay cone itself: decomposing $\langle F\rangle_\Omega$ by ingredient (quadrature refinement, slant-depth truncation, limb-grid resolution, cone shape at fixed second moment, the fixed-density approximation for $p(X,E)$) shows every one of them moves the horizon/vertical ratio by $\lesssim2\%$, while the cone width alone moves it by $\pm20\%$ for a $\pm25\%$ change in width. Since NA61/SHINE constrains the underlying $\langle\theta^2\rangle$ (Section 4.2) to $\pm12\%$, **this is the honest sub-GeV zenith-shape systematic — roughly $\pm20\%$ on horizon/vertical at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — and it alone spans the gap between Honda and Bartol** (Section 4.5). Below $\approx0.15\,\mathrm{GeV}$, where cones reach $\approx40^\circ$, the factorised geometry becomes an extrapolation and both measures should be read with that caveat; the high-energy limit is recovered exactly ($F_s\to1$).

**Consistency with the muon calibration.** The **full** factor is applied on every base, including the muon-calibrated `daemonflux` one, justified by an explicit closure check built into the table generation. The same off-axis machinery evaluated with the **muon** angular kernel (the generator pion angle folded with exact $\pi\to\mu\nu$ kinematics for the massive daughter) is consistent with unity in `daemonflux`'s muon-calibration region (vertical directions, $E_\mu\gtrsim 5\,\mathrm{GeV}$: $E_{\rm off}^\mu = 0.998$–$1.000$, unchanged by the corrected moments). `daemonflux`'s *neutrino* flux is itself genuinely 1D, and because $E_{\rm off}^\mu\simeq 1$ where the muon data live, the calibrated hadronic yields carry no hidden 3D normalisation for the full $E_{\rm off}^\nu$ to double-count; applying it therefore leaves the muon–neutrino calibration intact. A still-cleaner design would inject the calibration one level deeper — at the MCEq hadronic yields rather than the 1D flux output — so muons and neutrinos share one calibrated cascade; this is discussed in Section 6. The data-driven hadronic uncertainty (NA61 $\pm12\%$ on $\sigma_\pi$) is $\pm8\%$ on the excess at the $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ horizon, falling to $\sim\!0$ by a few GeV, and is delivered as a nuisance pull; it is evaluated to first order, as the ratio of two table evaluations rather than a re-evaluation of Eq. (8b) at $\sigma_\pi\pm12\%$ (Section 6).

### 3.7 Muon bending

Muons produced in the cascade bend in the geomagnetic field before they decay, so the decay neutrinos inherit a deflected direction. Over the in-flight decay the bending angle is

$$
\Delta\phi = \omega_c\,t_{\rm lab} = \frac{qB}{\gamma m_\mu}\cdot \gamma T_p = \frac{qB\,T_p}{m_\mu},
\tag{9}
$$

with $T_p$ the muon's *proper* time. Averaged over one lifetime this is $qB\tau_\mu/m_\mu = 5.08^\circ$ for the local field magnitude at Kamioka ($|B| = 0.474\,\mathrm{G}$). Eq. (9) is **exactly energy-independent** — the longer flight of an energetic muon is exactly offset by its larger gyroradius — and, less obviously, **exactly independent of energy loss**: writing $d\theta/ds = qB_\perp/p$ with $ds = \beta c\gamma\,dT_p$ and $p=\gamma m\beta c$ gives $d\theta = qB_\perp dT_p/m_\mu$ at every instant, whatever $dE/dx$ does to $p$ along the way (asserted to $10^{-6}$ with and without continuous $dE/dx$ in `test_muon_segment_mc.py`). Energy loss can only enter *indirectly*, by changing which muons can still make a neutrino of the requested energy. What depends on energy is whether the muon decays in flight at all, governed by the decay-in-flight fraction

$$
f_{\rm dec}(E_\mu,\theta_z) = 1 - \exp\!\left(-\,\frac{L(\theta_z)}{\gamma\beta c\tau_\mu}\right),
\tag{10}
$$

with $L(\theta_z)$ the curved-atmosphere slant path from the production altitude.

**How good is a single mean shift?** A dedicated backward Monte-Carlo of the muon segment (`muon_segment_mc.py`: the decay point sampled on the arrival ray, the muon direction generated backwards from the required neutrino direction through the exact boosted-Michel density, the proper time drawn from $\mathrm{Exp}(\tau_\mu)$, continuous PDG $dE/dx$, and the full IGRF-13 field evaluated along the muon's *own* trajectory) gives a mean bend of $4.76^\circ$/$4.71^\circ$/$4.59^\circ$ at $87$/$81$/$75^\circ$ — $0.86$–$0.97$ times the one-lifetime value, the reduction coming from energy loss forcing higher production energies on a steeply falling spectrum and so selecting *short* proper times. The distribution is exponential to within statistics (RMS/mean $\approx 1$; the $16/50/84$ percentiles are $0.18/0.69/1.81$ times the mean against the exponential's $0.17/0.69/1.83$). Propagating the full exponential through the cutoff map rather than a single mean shift changes the resulting charge-split cutoff response by $\le 1\%$ ($1.115$ vs $1.106$ at $87^\circ$ East; $1.153$ vs $1.146$ at $81^\circ$ East), and the exponential tail slightly *dilutes* rather than enhances the effect. A single coherent mean shift is therefore adequate, and is what the engine applies.

The shift is computed with the full local field vector (IGRF-13) and the actual muon direction: $\mu^+$ and $\mu^-$ deflect oppositely, and at $87^\circ$ East the $5.08^\circ$ decomposes into $3.17^\circ$ of zenith and $3.94^\circ$ of azimuth. Only the zenith part has leverage on the cutoff (the azimuth part is a rigid rotation common to East and West). This coherent, **charge-signed** shift is folded into the delivered flux through the **muon channel of the joint integral** (Eq. 8b): that channel's cutoff lookup is displaced by $\pm\boldsymbol{\delta}$ with the sign set by the parent muon charge ($\nu_\mu,\bar\nu_e$ from $\mu^-$; $\nu_e,\bar\nu_\mu$ from $\mu^+$), while the *production* stays on the unshifted primary because the bend happens after the shower has developed. The cone width used with it is the bending-free one, so the coherent shift and a bending floor cannot double-count. Because the four species are tracked separately (not charge-summed), each carries the full shift rather than the cancelled net — a genuinely charge-dependent directional effect relevant to $\nu/\bar\nu$ separation (Sections 4.1 and 6).

## 4. Validation

### 4.1 Absolute and directional flux versus Honda HKKM2014

We compare the absolute $\nu_\mu$ flux at Kamioka against the Honda HKKM2014 tables [6] on a dense energy grid from $0.1$ to $100\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Both predictions are log–log interpolated to common energies; a naïve nearest-grid-point comparison mismatches MCEq's $0.89\,\mathrm{GeV}$ node against Honda's $1.0\,\mathrm{GeV}$ bin and is misleading on a steep spectrum.

The comparison (Table 2, Figure 5) reveals the role of the 1D base. The raw-MCEq base lies $\approx 25$–$30\%$ below Honda across $0.3$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$, a genuine SIBYLL/H3a normalisation deficit. The `daemonflux`-anchored base agrees with Honda to $\approx 10\%$ for $E\gtrsim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ up to $100\,\mathrm{GeV}$, but over-predicts toward $0.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (up to $\sim\!2\times$), where it extrapolates beyond its muon calibration. The two bases **bracket Honda below $\sim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}$** (daemonflux above, MCEq below); above $\sim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ both lie below Honda, with the data-anchored daemonflux base the closer of the two.

The sub-GeV discrepancy of the raw-MCEq base is traced to the **primary spectrum, not the hadronic model**: the H3a parametrisation under-predicts the measured nucleon flux in the $\sim\!5$–$30\,\mathrm{GeV}$ parent region (by $\approx 32\%$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$) relative to the data-driven **Global Spline Fit** (GSF, fitted to AMS-02/BESS/PAMELA — precisely the dedicated low-energy dataset the sub-GeV flux needs). Re-running the same SIBYLL-2.3d cascade with the GSF primary brings the MCEq base into $\approx\pm10\%$ of Honda at $0.15$–$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, while the muon-calibrated daemonflux base remains superior above $\sim\!1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — the two are best in *complementary* domains with a crossover near $0.8\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The recommended configuration is therefore the **hybrid base** (`base_model="hybrid"` with the GSF primary): GSF-anchored MCEq below, muon-calibrated daemonflux above, blended smoothly over one octave at $E_0=1.7\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (`hybrid_weight`) — the daemonflux muon-calibration floor ($E_\mu\ge 5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, per its documentation) mapped to neutrinos via $E_\nu\simeq E_\mu/3$, a physics-derived (not fitted) crossover; a scan over $E_0\in[0.5,1.7]\,\mathrm{GeV}$ moves the delivered ratios by $\le 6\%$ below $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and confirms the derived value. Two further quantified items: seasonal atmospheric variation (MSIS00, January/July) changes the vertical $\nu_\mu$ flux by $\lesssim 0.3\%$ over $0.3$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (negligible for neutrinos), and the force-field knob now supports **de-modulation** ($\varphi<0$: $S(-0.35\,\mathrm{GV})=1.13$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$), enabling exact epoch matching to the solar-minimum reference tables. With the full engine this reproduces the Honda solar-minimum tables to **$0.90$–$1.10$ for all four species at every zenith over $0.15$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$** (Section 4.6; e.g. vertical $\nu_e$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$: $0.93$, versus $1.64$ with the `daemonflux` base alone) — Honda/Bartol-competitive across the full range. The inter-base spread remains the model systematic (Section 5), now between two much closer, independently-anchored models. (In these comparisons the reference epoch matters: the Honda Kamioka table used here is empirically pinned to **solar minimum** — it matches Bartol's solar-min tables to $\lesssim 4\%$ below $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ versus $7$–$17\%$ for solar-max — while the GSF epoch is a data-averaged one, leaving a $\sim\!\pm10\%$ sub-$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ epoch ambiguity that the solar-potential pull covers.)

**The East–West asymmetry, and how it must be measured.** Two conventions have to be fixed before any comparison. First, Honda's azimuth is measured *counterclockwise from South* (arXiv:1102.2688 § II), ours clockwise from North, so $\varphi_{\rm compass} = (180^\circ - \varphi_{\rm Honda})\bmod 360^\circ$: the two are mirror images about the East–West axis. Second, the legacy statistic $\max/\min$ over a uniform azimuth grid is **exactly even** under a sign flip of a coherent rotation of the azimuthal pattern, and so is structurally blind to the charge-dependent effect it was used to test; an earlier version of this work reported a null from it. Two candidate explanations are excluded quantitatively. The near-limb rise of the East cutoff is now resolved to $\le 0.06\,\mathrm{GV}$ by the dense zenith nodes (Section 3.3), so it is not an interpolation artefact; and Honda's cosine-zenith *bin averaging* is not an alibi either — averaged uniformly over the $0$–$0.1$ bin the East cutoff is $43.5\,\mathrm{GV}$ against $42.1$ at the bin centre, with the West unchanged, so a bin-averaged evaluation would give a *larger* amplitude, not a smaller one. The natural *data-level* out-of-sample test is a direct comparison against the Super-Kamiokande **measured** East–West asymmetry [26], which requires folding the detector response and is left to an analysis-level study.

### 4.2 Hadronic production versus NA61/SHINE

MCEq's *stored* interaction matrices are $p_T$-integrated ($\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}x_L$, no angular information), so we regenerate the **two-dimensional** production kernels $\mathrm{d}^2N/(\mathrm{d}x_L\,\mathrm{d}p_T)$ by re-running the same generators (SIBYLL-2.3d above $80\,\mathrm{GeV}$, UrQMD-3.4 below) through `chromo` in single-interaction mode and histogramming in $(x_L,\theta)$ (`kernel_regeneration.py`, `regen_moments_mp.py`); it is the second moment of this kernel that sets the cone widths of Section 3.6. Marginalising it over $p_T$ reproduces MCEq's stored $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}x_L$ to a normalisation of $0.97$–$1.00$ per species (`gate_moments_norm.py`, the pipeline's consistency gate). We validate the *angular* content against fixed-target data in two complementary ways.

The first is convention-blind: the mean transverse momentum $\langle p_T\rangle(p)$ of charged pions and kaons in $p+\mathrm{C}$ at $31\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$ against the NA61/SHINE measurements [18,19] (HEPData [22] records ins886780 and ins1397003, fetched via `hepdata-cli`), applying the experiment's angular acceptance. For pions the agreement is $\approx 10\%$ (Figure 7); for kaons UrQMD reproduces the $\langle p_T\rangle$ scale ($0.2$–$0.6\,\mathrm{GeV}$) and its rise with momentum, running $10$–$20\%$ harder than the data (Figure 8).

The second tests the production-angle definition itself, which $\langle p_T\rangle$ cannot: `validate_na61_angle.py` compares $\langle\theta\rangle(p_{\rm lab})$ and its RMS directly against NA61's polar-angle tables ($\theta<420\,\mathrm{mrad}$ acceptance, $1.2\times10^5$ events). Below $1\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$ — the region that drives the sub-GeV cone — the $\arcsin$ correction of Section 3.6 moves UrQMD/NA61 from $0.923$ to $0.965$ for $\pi^+$ and from $0.926$ to $0.967$ for $\pi^-$, i.e. it halves the discrepancy from $7.5\%$ low to $3.4\%$ low, with the RMS following ($0.927\to0.975$, $0.929\to0.977$). Above $1\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$ the two angle conventions differ by only $1$–$2\%$ and the residual is the pre-documented UrQMD forward-fragmentation behaviour ($\pi^+$ high, $\pi^-$ low), unrelated to this correction. The $\pm 12\%$ $\langle p_T\rangle$ uncertainty is propagated as the `sigma_pi_NA61` nuisance pull (Section 5).

### 4.5 The near-horizon 3D excess, from first principles

Unlike in earlier versions of this work, the excess is **not** flavour-independent. The cone is a property of the whole parent chain, and $\nu_e$ — essentially all of muon-decay origin — samples a wider cone than a directly produced $\nu_\mu$; the delivered $E_{\rm off}$ at the $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ horizon differs by $4\%$ between $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e$ (Section 3.6). The much larger $\nu_e$ *flux* horizon enhancement (horizon/vertical $\approx 2.3$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, rising to $\approx 5.0$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$) still arises mostly from the more horizon-peaked 1D $\nu_e$ base, which the engine applies per flavour; the species dependence of the cone is a $\lesssim 5\%$ modulation on top of it, but it is the modulation that carries the last step of Table 5.

### 4.6 Grid-wide comparison across all four species

The tests above are targeted observables. As a global check we evaluated the delivered flux on Honda's **exact** tabulation grid — all ten down-going $\cos\theta_z$ bins $\times$ twelve azimuth bins $\times$ $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — for all four charge/flavour species, and compared cell by cell (`diag_full_comparison.py`). Four results are worth recording.

**Absolute normalisation.** The azimuth-averaged ratio to Honda lies between $0.90$ and $1.10$ for *every* species at every zenith over $0.15$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The systematic $10$–$18\%$ near-horizon undershoot at $0.3$–$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ reported by an earlier version of this work is gone: the horizon column now runs $0.93$–$1.05$ for every species at every energy. The residual pattern is a $\approx 6$–$10\%$ deficit at $0.15$–$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (all zeniths, following the base) and a $\approx 4$–$6\%$ excess at $1$–$5\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Grid-wide, with the default `cutoff_anchor="prod_point"` (`diag_full_comparison.py`, `scratchpad/anchor/full_after.log`), the median $|\log_{10}(\text{ours}/\text{Honda})|$ is $0.022$–$0.036$ ($\approx 5$–$9\%$) per species, with a $90$th percentile of $0.061$–$0.090$ ($\approx 15$–$23\%$; the pre-anchor values were $0.066$–$0.101$, $\approx 16$–$26\%$ — every species' tail improves, medians unchanged); the tail is dominated by the near-horizon azimuthal *pattern* (Section 6), not by any normalisation offset. It is worth being explicit that the *bare* 1D base sits $2$–$3.5\times$ above Honda at $0.11$–$0.2\,\mathrm{GeV}$; this is not an excess in the delivered product but the expected consequence of Honda's tabulated flux already containing the Kamioka geomagnetic screening that $G$ supplies here.

**Flavour ratio.** $(\Phi_{\nu_e}+\Phi_{\bar\nu_e})/(\Phi_{\nu_\mu}+\Phi_{\bar\nu_\mu})$ tracks Honda to $\le 3\%$ at the vertical over the whole band and to $\le 8\%$ at the horizon (worst case $+8\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$) — the sharpest test of the $\pi\to\mu\to e$ chain, and independent of the absolute scale.

**Charge ratios.** $\nu_\mu/\bar\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e/\bar\nu_e$ agree with Honda to $\lesssim 4\%$ at the vertical over the full range. At the horizon the agreement is $\lesssim 8\%$, with a specific signature: the model's $\nu_e/\bar\nu_e$ runs low there ($1.12$ vs $1.20$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $1.18$ vs $1.26$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$) and its $\nu_\mu/\bar\nu_\mu$ runs high ($1.02$ vs $0.99$, $1.05$ vs $0.99$) — the azimuth-averaged shadow of the missing East–West charge splitting of Section 4.1, not an independent discrepancy.

**Independent cross-check.** Against Bartol — which is not used anywhere in the construction and is independent of Honda — the vertical flux agrees to $0.88$–$1.02$ for both $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e$ over $0.3$–$3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Section 4.4).

Two caveats limit how far this comparison can be pushed. First, SK-2016's own quoted asymmetry values do not exactly reproduce its published Fig. 22 histogram, so all numbers above are quoted as ratios to SK's own HKKM11 prediction, which is insensitive to that offset. Second, the flux-level comparison omits the event-level ingredients a real test needs: the $\nu\to$lepton opening-angle kernel (which dilutes $A$ by roughly $\times0.7$), the CCQE/RES/DIS cross-section mix with the correct $\nu/\bar\nu$ weighting, NC contamination, detector acceptance, and three-flavour oscillations on the up-going half. A cheaper and sharper target for a future, event-level study is SK's **dipole-phase rotation with zenith** (Fig. 23 of [SK-2016], a $2.2\sigma$ effect) — a shape observable largely immune to the smearing dilution, and one this engine already reproduces to $\le1.3^\circ$ relative to Honda (Section 6(ii)) after the production-point cutoff anchor.

## 5. Systematic uncertainties

It is important to separate two distinct sources here, which earlier drafts of this work conflated. The **interaction-model** uncertainty — the genuine ambiguity in the hadronic yields at fixed primary flux — is *small*: running SIBYLL-2.3d, EPOS-LHC, DPMJET-III and QGSJET-II through the identical setup (`hadronic_spread.py`) gives a spread of only $\approx 4$–$7\%$ sub-GeV (rising to $\approx 12$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV). Consistently, the two independent *full-3D* references agree far better than the historical folklore "$20$–$40\%$": Honda and Bartol track each other to $\approx 4$–$15\%$ across $0.1$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Section 4.4). The larger uncertainty that dominates *our* sub-GeV budget is instead the **overall-normalisation / primary-flux / calibration** ambiguity — historically the reason different groups' absolute fluxes scattered — which we quantify from the spread of our two independently data-anchored bases *around the recommended central*. The recommended central is the **GSF-anchored hybrid** $\Phi_{\rm hyb}$ (Section 4.1); the band is its half log-spread against the muon-calibrated daemonflux base $\Phi_{\rm df}$,

$$
\sigma_{\rm sys}(E) = \tfrac12\left|\ln\!\frac{\Phi_{\rm df}}{\Phi_{\rm hyb}}\right| ,\qquad
\Phi_{\rm central} \equiv \Phi_{\rm hyb} .
\tag{11}
$$

Recomputed around this recommended central, the band is $\pm 20\%$ at $0.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\pm 15\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\pm 14\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\pm 5\%$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and collapses to $\lesssim 1\%$ above the $\sim 2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ crossover (Figure 12) — roughly **half** the earlier raw-H3a-MCEq$\leftrightarrow$daemonflux band ($\pm 31\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$), which had inflated the sub-GeV uncertainty with the H3a-primary base ($\approx 25$–$30\%$ low over $0.3$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$) that we recommend against. This is the number to propagate through an oscillation fit as a correlated normalisation-versus-energy systematic. Two points make it coherent and complete. First, above the crossover $\Phi_{\rm hyb}\equiv\Phi_{\rm df}$, so this base-spread term vanishes *by construction*; the residual uncertainty there is carried by the two other pulls — the muon-calibration covariance ($\approx 3$–$5\%$) and the hadronic interaction-model spread ($\approx 10$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV, below) — which the delivered covariance folds in quadrature, so the total model uncertainty is never understated. Second, being a spread of two data-anchored models it is an *intra-framework* systematic; it does not by itself span other calculations, but Honda and Bartol (which agree to $\approx 4\%$, §4.4) both sit within it sub-GeV, and the direct Bartol comparison (§4.4) plus the hadronic-model spread (below) supply the inter-calculation ingredients. Sub-dominant, well-bounded effects include the production-angle treatment ($\sim 1$–$2\%$), the zenith-independence of $G_s$ ($\le 2\%$, Section 3.4), the composition-averaged bound $\langle A/Z\rangle$, and the muon-bending kinematic constants, which matter only for fine-grained charge-resolved studies.

The raw H3a-MCEq curve (faint) is shown for reference; the recomputed band around the hybrid central is roughly half the old H3a$\leftrightarrow$daemonflux envelope and collapses above the $\sim 2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ crossover.](base_comparison.png)

**Decomposing the spread: interaction model versus calibration.** To separate the hadronic-interaction contribution from the overall normalisation, we run the same base setup (H3a primary, US-Standard atmosphere) through the four hadronic models MCEq carries — SIBYLL-2.3d, EPOS-LHC, DPMJET-III-19.3, QGSJET-II-04 — a proxy for the inter-calculation (Bartol/FLUKA) spread whose dominant driver is the hadronic model. The **interaction-model spread is only $\approx 4$–$7\%$ sub-GeV, rising to $\approx 12$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV** (Figure 13). This is much smaller than the daemonflux$\leftrightarrow$MCEq spread below a few GeV, which establishes an important point: the **sub-GeV flux uncertainty is dominated by the overall normalisation/muon-calibration, not by the interaction model** — precisely why anchoring to muon data matters. At multi-GeV the two contributions become comparable ($\sim 10\%$ each). We caution that this MCEq-internal spread is *not* a substitute for a literal inter-code comparison — those codes differ also in primary flux, atmosphere and 3D treatment — but it bounds the hadronic-model part; the direct Bartol comparison (§4.4), which found Honda and Bartol consistent to $\approx 4\%$, supplies the complementary inter-code check, and a FLUKA table can be ingested through the same path when available.

The spread is $\approx 4$–$7\%$ sub-GeV, rising to $\approx 12$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV — smaller than the calibration-driven spread of Figure 12 below a few GeV.](hadronic_spread.png)

**Directional systematics: primary spectrum and hadronic-model envelope on the East–West amplitude.** The two systematics above bound the *absolute* base; a dedicated model/primary scan (nine interaction-model$\times$primary combinations, `diag_model_scan.py`, response-model overrides `gs_interaction_model`/`gs_primary` independent of the base) shows that the directional East–West observable of Section 4.1 carries a materially different, and previously unquantified, pull. Because the base is azimuth-flat, W/E is a pure cutoff-response$\times$cone observable, and the single-direction cutoff response $G(R_c{=}7.1\,\mathrm{GV})/G(R_c{=}42.3\,\mathrm{GV})$ at $87^\circ$/$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ is $4.64$ (SIBYLL-2.3d/GSF, delivered), $4.38$ (DPMJET-III), $4.18$ (EPOS-LHC), $4.01$ (SIBYLL-2.3d/H3a), $3.96$ (SIBYLL-2.3d/Gaisser–Honda): the **primary spectrum moves it $\approx-15\%$, the hadronic model at most $\approx-10\%$**. Propagated through the full $\nu_\mu$ W/E amplitude at $87^\circ$/$0.5$–$2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ against Honda, the primary choice alone removes $-9$ to $-11$ of the delivered $+12\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Gaisser–Honda $+1\%$, H3a $+3\%$, against $+12\%$ for the delivered GSF) and about half of the $+21$/$+25\%$ at $1$/$2\,\mathrm{GeV}$; we therefore carry a **primary-spectrum pull on the East–West amplitude of $\pm10$–$15\%$**, an order of magnitude larger than its effect on horizon/vertical ($\le1\%$). The hadronic-model envelope across the same scan is $\le7$ points on the East–West amplitude (EPOS-LHC the outlier), $\le2\%$ on horizon/vertical, and $\le2\%$ on the $\nu_e/\bar\nu_e$ and $\nu_\mu/\bar\nu_\mu$ charge ratios — consistent with the structural finding that MCEq's shipped database splices DPMJET-III-19.3 (Honda's own generator family) into every model below $\approx80\,\mathrm{GeV}$ projectile energy, making the hadronic-model lever nearly vacuous for sub-GeV neutrinos. Neither lever moves the charge-ratio *pattern* itself ($\nu_e/\bar\nu_e$ stays at $1.07$–$1.08$ with muon bending off across every model and primary tried, against Honda's $2.24$), which is why that residual is attributed to the cone average (Section 6(i)) rather than to an input choice. Separately, the NA61 pion-angle pull of the previous paragraph, quantified through the cone width rather than the base, moves horizon/vertical by $\approx\pm20\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ for its measured $\pm12\%$ on $\langle\theta^2\rangle$ (Section 3.6) — an order of magnitude larger than either the interaction-model or the primary-spectrum effect on that same ratio, and the dominant sub-GeV zenith-shape uncertainty.

**Solar modulation.** A distinct, *time-dependent* effect is the 11-year solar cycle, which modulates the sub-GeV primary flux. We include it as an optional Gleeson–Axford force-field knob on the primary (`solar_factor`, `solve(solar_modulation=φ)`): the potential $\phi$ [GV] is applied per nucleon with the rigidity-dependent energy loss ($Z/A\,\phi$; free protons lose the full $\phi$, bound nucleons half), and the resulting neutrino-energy factor $S(E)$ — obtained through the cascade, hence species- and $\sim$zenith-independent like $G_s$ — rescales the flux. Taking $\phi\approx 0.4\,\mathrm{GV}$ (solar minimum) and $\approx 1.0\,\mathrm{GV}$ (solar maximum), the predicted solar-cycle amplitude $\Phi(\mathrm{min})/\Phi(\mathrm{max})$ is $+22\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $+14\%$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $+2\%$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Figure 14), consistent with the $\sim 10$–$20\%$ sub-GeV variation reported by Honda and Bartol. The potential is defined relative to the H3a baseline ($\phi=0$); the physical observable is the difference between two potentials, so a time-integrated analysis should either fix $\phi$ to the data-taking epoch or carry the min–max band as a systematic.

**Propagated muon-calibration uncertainty.** The model-spread band above is a *between-model* systematic; distinct from it is daemonflux's *within-model* nuisance-parameter covariance — the calibrated error budget that is the 1D model's defining strength. Because $\Phi_{3D}=\Phi_{\rm df}\,F_s\,S$ with $F_s$ and $S$ independent of the daemonflux parameters, both the 1-$\sigma$ error and the full parameter covariance carry through by scaling each daemonflux gradient by $G\,S$; in particular the fractional error is unchanged. We propagate it with `solve(with_calib_error=True)`, returning the absolute $1\sigma$ (`flux_err`) and fractional (`flux_relerr`) calibration uncertainty per species, with `calib_hadronic_only` isolating the hadronic-production component. At Kamioka the propagated calibration uncertainty on $\nu_\mu$ is $\approx 3$–$5\%$ over $0.3$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (hadronic-dominated), and the preservation $\sigma(\Phi_{3D})/\Phi_{3D}=\sigma(\Phi_{\rm df})/\Phi_{\rm df}$ is verified numerically to $<0.1\%$ (Figure 15). For a fit that needs the *correlated* nuisance structure rather than the diagonal band, `with_calib_jacobian=True` returns the 24 daemonflux nuisance-parameter names, their correlation matrix, and the fractional per-parameter Jacobian $R_i=\partial\ln\Phi/\partial\eta_i$ (identical for base and directional flux); `calib_covariance` then assembles the full energy–energy covariance $\mathrm{Cov}=(\Phi R)^\top\!\rho\,(\Phi R)$ for any direction. This reproduces daemonflux's `error()` to machine precision ($<10^{-11}$) and yields the expected correlations (e.g. $\rho(1,3\,\mathrm{GeV})=0.99$, $\rho(1,30\,\mathrm{GeV})=0.67$), so an oscillation fit can carry daemonflux's pulls as correlated nuisance parameters directly on the 3D flux. This calibration band ($\sim$few percent) and the model-spread band (Figure 12, larger sub-GeV) are complementary and should both enter an analysis; the former is the appropriate within-daemonflux systematic, the latter the base-choice/extrapolation uncertainty.

**One covariance for everything.** The remaining systematics are likewise delivered as unit-variance nuisance pulls appended to the same parameter set, so a single call returns the *complete* error model: `with_eoff_jacobian=True` adds the hadronic off-axis pull `sigma_pi_NA61` (the NA61 $\pm12\%$ pion-angle response, $\approx\pm8\%$ on the sub-GeV horizon excess and, propagated through the cone width, $\approx\pm20\%$ on horizon/vertical at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — the dominant sub-GeV zenith-shape systematic, Section 5; $\to 0$ by a few GeV; evaluated to first order, Section 6 (vi)); `solar_sigma_gv` adds a solar-potential pull of the chosen $1\sigma$; and `with_base_spread=True` adds the fully-correlated base-model-choice pull (the half log-spread between the MCEq and daemonflux bases — the dominant sub-GeV systematic of Section 5). These pulls are mutually uncorrelated and uncorrelated with the 24 calibration parameters (block-diagonal correlation); `calib_covariance` then assembles the full energy–energy covariance including all of them, and `flux_relerr`/`flux_err` fold them in quadrature. An oscillation fit therefore carries calibration, hadronic-production-angle, solar and model-choice uncertainties as named, correlated nuisance parameters on the directional flux — the daemonflux error philosophy extended to every 3D ingredient.

## 6. Discussion and limitations

We are explicit about the trust boundary. The delivered engine (Section 3, Eq. 1) is the complete deterministic-3D construction — curved per-zenith cascade $\times$ the joint production-and-cutoff cone integral $\times$ solar — validated absolutely against Honda and Bartol and at the kinematic level against NA61. The large genuinely-3D **sub-GeV near-horizon excess** (horizon/vertical $\approx 1.9$ for $\nu_\mu$ and $\approx 2.3$ for $\nu_e$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, Section 4.5) is produced *from first principles*: MCEq depth-resolved production, curved geometry, and generator production angles folded with exact decay, with no hand-set hadronic parameters and no reference-flux input. It is now **species-dependent**, because the cone is the distribution of the primary direction given the *neutrino* direction and therefore carries the whole decay chain; an earlier version of this work asserted the opposite and is retracted in Section 3.6. The uncertainty on it is data-driven: the NA61 $\pm12\%$ on the pion production angle propagates to $\pm8\%$ on the sub-GeV horizon excess; the complementary multi-generator (EPOS/QGSJET) spread requires regenerating the moments with those generators.

We note that the neutrino part of Eq. (8b) **is** the exact straight-line 3D neutrino transport, not a factorisation of it: because neutrinos do not scatter, the full coupled transport from production to detector is the altitude-resolved cone average, which is precisely what the engine evaluates. An independent from-scratch ray-traced re-implementation (`mceq3d_prodvertex.py`, taking the production profile from a separate hand-march of MCEq's real matrices) reproduces the delivered off-axis factor to $\approx 1$–$2\%$, with both gates passing exactly (the factor $\to 1$ as the cone width $\to 0$, to $5\times10^{-4}$, and $\to 1$ at high energy). The only genuinely-3D terms the construction omits for neutrinos are charged-shower lateral development and the coupling between the directional cutoff and shower development.

**The remaining limitations, in order of size.** (Item (ii) below, listed in an earlier draft, has since been resolved and is kept in place with its resolution recorded, rather than renumbering the list.)

**(i) The East–West species pattern is the principal limitation.** Honda orders the four species' West/East amplitude $\nu_e\,(4.74) > \bar\nu_\mu\,(3.81) > \nu_\mu\,(2.51) > \bar\nu_e\,(2.12)$ at $87^\circ$ and $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$; the delivered engine compresses all four onto $2.7$–$3.3$ (Table 3). The *unsplit level* is right — with the muon bending switched off all four collapse onto $2.88$–$3.10$, inside the range Honda spans and $7$–$13\%$ below his four-species mean of $3.3$ — and the *phase* half of the charge splitting is largely reproduced ($60$–$145\%$ of Honda's, Table 4). What is missing is the amplitude: the clean splitting observable $[\bar\nu/\nu]_{\rm E}/[\bar\nu/\nu]_{\rm W}$, which cancels the intrinsic flavour ratio and the normalisation and equals unity if the bending does nothing, reaches only $15\%$ ($\nu_\mu$ pair) and $24\%$ ($\nu_e$ pair) of Honda's at the horizon, rising to $\approx 40\%$ by $\cos\theta_z=0.45$. We have tested and excluded every mechanism available inside the factorised construction. *Scaling the coherent shift*: multiplying the charge-signed displacement by $5$–$8$ does reproduce Honda's species pattern at $87^\circ$ ($\nu_e$ $4.31$ at $\times5$ and $5.02$ at $\times8$ against Honda's $4.74$), but the response is sub-linear, so the linear-regime equivalent is a coherent zenith shift of $\pm20^\circ$ against the $\pm3.17^\circ$ that one muon lifetime supplies — unphysical, and we do not adopt it. *Cone reshaping*: scaling the muon-decay cone width over $0.4$–$1.25$ moves all four species' amplitudes together (the $\nu_\mu$ amplitude runs $3.34\to2.81$) and changes the $\nu_e/\bar\nu_e$ ratio only from $1.29$ to $1.19$. *Penumbra width*: sharpening $\sigma_{\ln R}$ from the legacy $0.5$ to the measured $0$ lifts every species by a nearly uniform $12$–$15$ points, improving $\nu_e$ and $\bar\nu_\mu$ and worsening $\nu_\mu$ and $\bar\nu_e$ by the same amount; the mean absolute bias over the four species is unchanged. *Production altitude*: moving $h_{\rm prod}$ from $5$ to $100\,\mathrm{km}$ moves the East $\mu^-/\mu^+$ cutoff ratio from $1.163$ to $1.138$. *Energy-loss-enhanced bending*: refuted, analytically and numerically (Section 3.7) — the bend is $qB\,T_p/m_\mu$ whatever $dE/dx$ does, and the full muon-segment Monte-Carlo gives a correction of $-3\%$ to $-14\%$ near the horizon, the wrong sign for the hypothesis, recovering $\lesssim8\%$ of the missing $\nu_e$ amplitude and none of the $\nu_\mu$ overshoot. The diagnosis that survives is that the *cone average*, not the bending mechanism, destroys the differentiation: the single-direction cutoff response at $87^\circ$/$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ is $3.79$/$4.08$/$4.35$/$3.45$ for $\nu_\mu$/$\bar\nu_\mu$/$\nu_e$/$\bar\nu_e$ — Honda's ordering and rough magnitude — and the cone average compresses it. The leverage is measurably small for a structural reason: $71\%$ of the East-bin muon-channel numerator comes from cone samples whose primary sits at a production-frame zenith below $75^\circ$, where $dR_c/d\theta_z$ is $0.4$–$0.6\,\mathrm{GV/deg}$, while the near-limb core that has $2.35\,\mathrm{GV/deg}$ carries $3\%$ of the flux; and on the West side $dR_c/d\theta_z\le0.2\,\mathrm{GV/deg}$ at $87^\circ$, so the model can produce essentially *no* West-side splitting where Honda's is $\approx\pm17\%$. We judge this beyond the factorised construction: a rigid mean shift applied to an isotropic Gaussian cone cannot represent the joint distribution of (primary direction, production point) given the neutrino direction, energy and parent charge. It is the first target of a genuine 3D Monte-Carlo, and users requiring charge-separated directional fluxes at the extreme horizon should treat the species ordering as unmodelled. We have since confirmed this diagnosis two independent ways. It is **not a different input**: scanning nine hadronic-model/primary combinations (Section 5) leaves the charge-ratio pattern untouched — $\nu_e/\bar\nu_e$ (bending off) sits at $1.07$–$1.08$ for every model and primary against Honda's $2.24$, a $\pm1\%$ spread of a required $+108\%$ shift, and the mechanism cannot even be probed below $\approx80\,\mathrm{GeV}$ projectile energy because MCEq's shipped tables are bit-identical to DPMJET-III there. And it is **corroborated by real data**: the Super-Kamiokande East–West asymmetry (Section 4.7) wants *more* $\nu_e$ and *less* $\nu_\mu$ splitting than Honda's own Monte Carlo, exactly the direction our species-compressed engine also fails to reach, at $\approx2$–$2.2\sigma$ with no free parameters. Two independent flux calculations (HKKM2014, Lipari [9]) and now sub-GeV SK data agree on the ordering $\nu_e>\bar\nu_\mu>\nu_\mu>\bar\nu_e$ that our cone average erases, which is why we regard this limitation as settled in kind — genuinely ours, not an artefact of the Honda comparison — even though its cure (a real 3D Monte-Carlo of the coupled cone) remains future work.

**(ii) A species-independent rotation of the azimuthal dipole — RESOLVED.** An earlier draft of this work reported the model's azimuthal pattern rotated as a whole relative to Honda's: at $87^\circ$ and $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the model/Honda ratio was $0.86$–$0.92$ at the geomagnetic North bin and $1.07$–$1.23$ at the South bin for *all four* species (a $\approx\pm25\%$ North-versus-South mismatch with no charge dependence), equivalently a $12$–$14^\circ$ clockwise phase offset at every energy and species. The investigation that closed it found two things. First, the apparent $\approx20^\circ$ rotation of the back-traced cutoff's own azimuthal pattern is *not* a frame, axis or convention error: every IGRF, declination, handedness and azimuth convention in the back-tracer checks out against a pure aligned dipole (vertical cutoff $=14.9\cos^4\lambda$ to $1.5\%$, correct Stormer East–West sign), and repeating the back-traced map in that same pure dipole — where geomagnetic East *is* azimuth $90^\circ$ by construction, with no declination or tilt to confuse the picture — reproduces the identical skew (first-harmonic phase $69.7^\circ$ against a Stormer-exact $90.0^\circ$). The map's cutoff is $R_U$, the *highest forbidden* rigidity, and above the Stormer main cone the Earth-shadow cone suppresses the North sector's $R_U$ far more than it moves the East peak, biasing a single first-harmonic fit even though the map's actual maximum sits exactly at geomagnetic East; a genuine but small bug (the geomagnetic north pole computed at $107.32^\circ$E instead of the published $80.59^\circ$N/$72.68^\circ$W) was fixed en route but changes the map by $\le0.56\,\mathrm{GV}$ and no quoted number here. Second, and this is the real remaining physics: the cutoff map is anchored at the **detector**, whereas the primary of a near-horizon neutrino enters the atmosphere at the production point, up to $617\,\mathrm{km}$ away at $h_{\rm prod}=30\,\mathrm{km}$, at a measurably different geomagnetic latitude — launching the back-trace from there instead moves the phase by $+10.6^\circ$ and the North/South contrast by $-29\%$ at $87^\circ$, the same size and sign as the model-versus-Honda gap. Anchoring the cutoff of every cone sample at the production point (`cutoff_anchor="prod_point"`, Section 3.3, now the delivered default) closes essentially the whole discrepancy with no free parameter: the North/South pair moves to $0.93$–$1.02$ / $0.91$–$1.05$ and the phase offset to $\le1.3^\circ$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ for all four species, confirmed grid-wide (every species' $90$th-percentile $|\log_{10}(\text{ours}/\text{Honda})|$ improves, Section 4.6). Because it acts on the site half of the geometry common to every cone sample it is exactly zero at the vertical and, as expected, does not touch the East–West species pattern of limitation (i) or the amplitude splitting of Table 4 — both are the cone average, not the cutoff anchor. One approximation remains from the fix itself: the bilinear interpolation across the $3\times3$ site family has measured $8$–$22\%$ outliers in the low-cutoff West/South-West cells (median cell $\approx1\%$), which a finer $5\times5$ family would quarter at $\approx4\times$ the one-time build cost; the fast `cutoff_anchor="detector"` path (rotate the frame only, $\approx1$–$2\,\mathrm{GV}$ approximate) is kept for A/B use.

**(iii) A horizon/vertical overshoot against Bartol — a cone-width uncertainty, not a conservation defect.** At $0.3$–$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the delivered horizon/vertical lands on Honda ($1.01$–$1.07$) but sits $6$–$13\%$ above Bartol (Table 5), and Honda and Bartol themselves differ by $9\%$ there, so "agrees with Honda" is not the same as "correct". An earlier draft of this work read the $1.046$–$1.065$ plain solid-angle average of Section 3.6 as a genuine net enhancement — flux "manufactured" by the construction — and offered it as the likely cause. A first-principles re-derivation shows this reading is **wrong**: Eq. (8) is the exact straight-line 3D transport of an isotropic primary flux, gated to reproduce its own flat-atmosphere closed form to $0.02$–$0.12\%$; the physically conserved quantity is the ground-crossing (cosine-weighted) average, not the plain solid-angle one, and in that measure the delivered factor is a $5.5\%$ *deficit*, while the same exact kernel gives $1.15$, not $1.046$, for the plain-$\Omega$ average in a flat atmosphere. **Nothing is manufactured.** Decomposing the horizon/vertical ratio by every ingredient of the construction (quadrature, slant-depth truncation, limb resolution, cone shape at fixed second moment, the fixed-density approximation) shows all of them move it by $\lesssim2\%$; the cone width alone moves it by $\pm20\%$ for a $\pm25\%$ change. Since NA61/SHINE constrains the underlying $\langle\theta^2\rangle$ to $\pm12\%$ (Section 4.2), the Bartol overshoot sits comfortably inside this **NA61 cone-width pull, which is now carried explicitly as the dominant sub-GeV zenith-shape systematic** (Section 5) rather than left as an unexplained excess. We do not adjust the cone width to close the gap, which would be tuning; we instead report the pull's size, $\approx\pm20\%$ on horizon/vertical at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, as the uncertainty that already spans it.

**(iv) A $\nu_\mu$ East–West overshoot at $1$–$2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — mostly a different input, with a genuine residual.** At $87^\circ$ the charge-summed $\nu_\mu$ amplitude overshoots Honda by $+19\%$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $+24\%$ at $2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Table 3, `cutoff_anchor="prod_point"`; $+23\%$/$+26\%$ pre-anchor), against $+10\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ ($+15\%$ pre-anchor). It resisted every step of the repair, the production-point anchor included (Section 6(ii) closes a different, North/South discrepancy and moves this one by only $\approx1$-$5$ points): the joint cone integral removed the $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ overshoot almost entirely ($+15\%\to+2\%$ at $\sigma_{\ln R}=0.5$) but left the $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ value unchanged, because the cone has largely closed by then and there is little covariance left to recover; and sharpening the penumbra to its measured value made it *worse* ($+12\%\to+23\%$). Its species dependence — $\nu_\mu$ and $\bar\nu_e$ overshoot while $\nu_e$ and $\bar\nu_\mu$ undershoot, by nearly the same amount — identifies it as part of limitation (i) rather than an independent defect.

A model/primary scan (Section 5) has since shown that most of this is not an engine defect at all but the choice of **primary spectrum**. At $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, replacing the delivered Global Spline Fit primary with Honda's own Gaisser–Honda proxy removes essentially the entire overshoot ($+12\%\to+1\%$ at $87^\circ$; the hadronic model contributes at most a further $-5$ points, EPOS-LHC the outlier). At $1$–$2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the primary removes about half of the excess ($+21\%\to+10\%$, $+25\%\to+14$–$16\%$), and a **genuine $+10$–$16\%$ residual survives every primary and hadronic model tried** — that remainder, not the full $+19$–$24\%$ quoted above under the delivered default, is the part of limitation (iv) that is still unexplained. We therefore keep the sharp penumbra (it is what the back-tracer measures) and now attribute most of the quoted overshoot to the primary-spectrum choice rather than to the construction, carrying $\pm10$–$15\%$ on the East–West amplitude as a distinct primary-spectrum systematic (Section 5).

**(v) The coupled deterministic march is a dead end, not future work.** Earlier versions of this work presented an angular-binned operator-splitting march of MCEq's real matrices with an in-cascade Lorentz-force operator (`mceq3d_real.march_checkpoints`) as the route to the charge-dependent East–West. We have now fixed its two numerical defects (a factor-ten gyroradius unit error and a non-bijective nearest-neighbour resample, replaced by a row-normalised inverse-square interpolation that is exact at zero rotation) and run it over the whole down-going hemisphere with the real charge-signed rotation on $\pi^\pm$, $K^\pm$ and $\mu^\pm$ at every checkpoint. It moves the dipole-phase splitting by $\le 0.3^\circ$ and the transverse dipole component by $\sim\!0.001$ — a few percent of Honda's, at the level of interpolation noise. The reason is structural, not numerical: near the horizon the atmospheric columns a bent muon moves between are hundreds of kilometres apart, but an angular-binned operator exchanges only direction labels at a common altitude, and no checkpoint refinement can repair that. The factorised shift of Section 3.7, which re-reads the cutoff at the displaced primary direction, is the physically correct cheap treatment; the honest expensive treatment is a genuine Monte-Carlo. We record this route as closed.

**(vi) The hadronic nuisance pull is first-order.** The `sigma_pi_NA61` pull is derived as the ratio of two evaluations of the tabulated off-axis factor at $\sigma_\pi\pm12\%$, not by re-evaluating the joint integral of Eq. (8b) at the shifted width. The two agree at $G\equiv1$ by construction, so the approximation is the neglect of the $\sigma_\pi$-dependence of the production–cutoff covariance; it is first-order correct and is flagged as such in the code.

Other known approximations, each bounded above: (a) the production-point cutoff is evaluated by rotating into the production-point frame and reading the detector-site map, not by launching a back-trace from the displaced point — the residual position/altitude term is $\approx 1$–$2\,\mathrm{GV}$; (b) up-going neutrino directions still take a single far-side cutoff, with no cone extension of their own, and fall back to the factorised product of Eq. (1b); (c) the depth-resolved production profile is taken as universal in slant depth, dropping the zenith-dependent $\rho(X)$ decay/interaction competition ($\approx 1$–$2\%$); (d) the high-statistics SIBYLL kernels degrade near the generator's soft-pion centre-of-mass threshold, and the $x_L$ clipping at $10^{-4}$ piles up genuinely soft secondaries in the first bin (worth $2$–$13\%$ on the pooled angular moment, present identically before and after the $\arcsin$ correction); (e) meson re-interactions are neglected (sub-GeV-negligible); and (f) the muon charge ratio entering the bending weight is fixed rather than derived.

**On the factorised architecture and where the calibration is injected.** The construction is deliberately factorised — $\Phi_{1D}\times F_s\times S$ — for speed, per-factor validatability, and to inherit `daemonflux`'s calibration and covariance; the full-3D references instead solve one coupled Monte-Carlo. Two distinct approximations follow. The first, the deterministic surrogate for native 3D transport, is quantified above. The second is subtler and worth stating plainly: we inject the muon calibration at the level of `daemonflux`'s **1D flux output** and multiply corrections onto it, whereas the calibration physically lives in geometry-agnostic **hadronic yields** (the Barr-style $\pi/K$ production parameters and the GSF primary). The cleaner architecture would apply those calibrated yields to the MCEq cascade directly, so that muons and neutrinos are produced by a single calibrated 3D cascade and the muon–neutrino consistency is automatic rather than checked *post hoc*. The GSF primary is already injected at this level (the hybrid base, Section 4.1); the hadronic (Barr) part is not, because the public `daemonflux` package ships flux-gradient splines and the parameter covariance but not the parameter$\to$yield recipe needed to reproduce it inside an arbitrary cascade. In its absence, the muon closure of Section 3.6 ($E_{\rm off}^\mu\simeq 1$ in the calibration region) is what guarantees the flux-level factorisation does not corrupt the calibration; the yield-level refit is the natural route to a genuinely unified (rather than corrected-1D) directly-calibrated 3D model.

**Performance.** On a single core, one MCEq cascade solve takes $\approx 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$. The cost of a directional solve is dominated by three one-time, cacheable ingredients: the full-sphere cutoff map (a cold build is $\approx 2500\,\mathrm{s}$ for the down-going hemisphere and $\approx 6200\,\mathrm{s}$ for the up-going one, run concurrently, per site and date), the cascade response $G_s$, which is site- and zenith-independent, and — with the default `cutoff_anchor="prod_point"` — the displaced-site cutoff-map family of Section 3.3, a further $\approx4.1\,\mathrm{h}$ on $46$ cores per site and date, cached alongside the detector map (`cutoff_anchor="detector"` skips this and reproduces the pre-2026-09-05 cost exactly). With all three warm, a full four-zenith $\times$ eight-azimuth solve including the joint cone integral takes $\approx 4\,\mathrm{min}$, of which the channel-resolved cone split is essentially free ($1.01\times$ the single-cone cost, because the expensive slant-depth lookup and ray quadrature are shared across channels), and a warm re-evaluation of a cached configuration loads in milliseconds. The framework is therefore a one-time per-site precompute followed by near-instant evaluation, in contrast to the CPU-weeks of a full 3D Monte-Carlo. Since the delivered down-going path never reads the up-going map, building only the down-going hemisphere would halve future site builds.

The `daemonflux` **nuisance-parameter covariance** is fully propagated to the directional flux (Sections 2 and 5): both the $1\sigma$ calibration band and the *full correlated covariance* (the 24 pulls, their correlation matrix, and the per-parameter Jacobian) are exported, reproducing `daemonflux`'s `error()` to machine precision, so a global fit can carry `daemonflux`'s calibrated, correlated nuisance parameters directly on the 3D flux. One capability remains deliberately out of scope: the **correlated muon** accompanying a down-going neutrino — the atmospheric self-veto passing fraction relevant to neutrino telescopes — is an event-level quantity that an inclusive flux model (this work, MCEq, or `daemonflux`) cannot provide; it requires a dedicated calculation such as nuVeto [25].

The seasonal and site dependence of the atmosphere is available through the NRLMSISE-00 profiles exposed by MCEq, but has not been studied here (seasonal variation of the vertical $\nu_\mu$ flux is $\lesssim 0.3\%$, Section 4.1). The former high-value extension — anchoring the sub-$0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ region to a dedicated low-energy dataset — is realised through the GSF-primary hybrid base (Section 4.1), which ties the sub-GeV central value to the AMS-02/BESS/PAMELA primary data rather than to an extrapolation. The natural next step for the directional physics is not a better factorisation but a lightweight genuine 3D Monte-Carlo, sampling primaries on an injection sphere with the same IGRF back-trace acceptance and the same SIBYLL-2.3d used here, and delivering per-site $\Phi_{3D}/\Phi_{1D}$ ratio tables computed on the same showers; that is the only route that can settle limitation (i) without model-versus-model circularity.

## 7. Conclusions

We have presented a fast, deterministic, data-anchored framework that extends the 1D `daemonflux` model into a fully directional, absolute, all-flavour atmospheric-neutrino flux over $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Each ingredient is either taken from a trusted source or validated against data: the absolute scale is anchored to muon-calibrated splines and to the Global Spline Fit primary; the geomagnetic cutoff is a first-principles full-IGRF back-trace located by a $\le1\,\mathrm{GV}$ ladder plus bisection on a limb-refined map, with a transmission that is the sharp step the back-tracer actually measures; the off-axis production geometry and the cutoff are evaluated in a single joint cone integral, channel-resolved and therefore species-dependent; and the production kinematics match NA61/SHINE in both $\langle p_T\rangle$ and, after the correction of Section 3.6, the production angle itself. On Honda's full grid the delivered flux agrees to $0.90$–$1.10$ for all four species, and the sub-GeV horizon excess is reproduced within, or close to, the Honda–Bartol mutual spread. The irreducible sub-GeV uncertainty is quantified as an explicit systematic band, and the calibration covariance is propagated exactly. The principal limitation is the four-species ordering of the near-horizon East–West amplitude: the charge-dependent mechanism is present with the correct sign and most of its phase signature, but only $15$–$25\%$ of its amplitude, and we have excluded every lever available inside the factorised construction rather than tuning any of them. The framework is suitable for oscillation analyses that require a directional flux at an arbitrary site with a transparent, propagatable error model, and provides a fast complement to full 3D Monte-Carlo calculations.

We particularly invite expert feedback on the choice and sub-GeV extrapolation of the 1D base; the cascade-correct definition of the geomagnetic suppression and the per-nucleus rigidity split; the residual net solid-angle excess of the production factor and what it implies about the muon-decay cone width; the far-side treatment of the up-going hemisphere; and whether the model-spread systematic is an appropriate way to present the irreducible low-energy uncertainty to an oscillation analysis.

We particularly invite expert feedback on the choice and sub-GeV extrapolation of the 1D base; the cascade-correct definition of the geomagnetic suppression and the per-nucleus rigidity split; the far-side treatment of the up-going hemisphere; and whether the model-spread systematic is an appropriate way to present the irreducible low-energy uncertainty to an oscillation analysis.

## Appendix A: numerical methods

The cascade solutions use MCEq with its default 1D matrix solver on a logarithmic energy grid of $\approx 10$ bins per decade ($\Delta\ln E\approx0.115$) from $0.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The trajectory back-tracing (Eq. 3) integrates with a fixed-step RK4 in arc length; the escape radius and step length are chosen so that the recovered Størmer cutoff is stable to better than $1\%$. The upper cutoff is located by a coarse top-down ladder of step $\le 1\,\mathrm{GV}$ from $r_{\rm hi}=55\,\mathrm{GV}$ followed by bisection of the [last allowed, first forbidden] bracket to $0.1\,\mathrm{GV}$; cells forbidden already at $r_{\rm hi}$ are flagged as saturated rather than clamped. The cutoff sky map is precomputed on a $42\times25$ $(\theta_z,\varphi)$ grid with dense zenith nodes at the limb and cached as a compressed array keyed on the site, date, ceiling and scan scheme; evaluations bilinearly interpolate it. With the default `cutoff_anchor="prod_point"`, a further $3\times3$ Cartesian family of such maps is built once per site and date at ground offsets $(d_{\rm north},d_{\rm east})\in\{-D,0,+D\}^2$, $D=617\,\mathrm{km}$ (the centre node reused from the detector map), using a seeded band scan that starts each displaced-site direction's rigidity search from the same direction's already-known detector cutoff; evaluations interpolate this family bilinearly in the site offset only, on top of the direction-axis interpolation above. The cutoff transmission of Eq. (7) is averaged analytically over the primary energy bin, which is what keeps the $\sigma_{\ln R}\to0$ limit continuous in $R_c$. The joint cone integral of Eq. (8b) uses a $44$-point polar $\times$ $18$-point azimuthal cone quadrature and a $260$-point ray quadrature, with the per-zenith production side memoised across azimuths and all parent channels stacked into one slant-depth lookup. The systematic band (Eq. 11) is evaluated pointwise in energy from the two base predictions.

## Appendix B: code and data availability

All code, unit tests, and figure-generating scripts are available on the fork `github.com/pgranger23/daemonflux`, branch `3d-extension`, under `tools/mceq3d/`; `ARCHITECTURE.md` there is the authoritative map of which modules are on the delivered path and which are research scaffolding. The absolute engine is `mceq3d_flux.py`; the joint production-and-cutoff cone integral of Eq. (8b) is `joint_cone.py`; the production profiles, the per-species channel decomposition and the legacy off-axis tables are built by `offaxis_mc.py`; the generator production-angle kernels and the exact muon-decay cone are in `kinematic_kernel.py`, regenerated by `kernel_regeneration.py` / `regen_moments_mp.py`; the geomagnetic back-tracer and cached cutoff are in `geomag_backtrace.py` (the sky map of Figure 1 is `make_cutoff_map_fig.py`). A bare `solve(lat, lon, cos_zenith, azimuth, use_cache=True, cache_dir=..., date=...)` reproduces every delivered number quoted here: `offaxis=True`, `cone_cutoff=True` (hence `joint_cone=True`), `joint_channels=True`, `cone_kernel="moments"` on the corrected v2 moments, `sublimb="prod_point"`, `cutoff_anchor="prod_point"` (default since 2026-09-05, Section 3.3; `"detector"` is the fast A/B path), `muon_bending=True` and `sigma_lnr=0` are all defaults, and `offaxis=False` selects the fast geomagnetic-only path. The validation scripts are `validate_honda.py`, `validate_ew_zenith.py` (the out-of-sample East–West zenith scan of Section 4.1), `diag_full_comparison.py` (the grid-wide comparison of Section 4.6), `diag_ew_charge_fourier.py` (the shift-sensitive East–West observable and Honda's azimuth mapping — any future per-azimuth comparison against Honda must go through it), `diag_shape_decompose.py`, `diag_joint_stages.py` (the cumulative ladder of Section 4.5), `diag_dipole_phase.py` and `diag_cutoff_anchor.py` (the dipole-phase investigation and the production-point-anchor validation of Section 6(ii), `test_cutoff_anchor.py` its unit tests), `validate_na61.py`, `validate_na61_angle.py`, `validate_na61_kaon.py`, `validate_bartol.py`, `base_comparison.py`, `hadronic_spread.py`, `solar_modulation.py`, `calib_uncertainty.py`, `diag_penumbra_width.py` (the measured penumbra of Table 1), `diag_eoff_conservation.py`, `gate_moments_norm.py`, `geomag_zenith_check.py` and `latitude_check.py`; `muon_segment_mc.py` is the closed muon-segment study of Section 3.7 and is not imported by the engine. `diag_model_scan.py` (Section 5) is the hadronic-model/primary/geomagnetic-epoch scan behind the primary-spectrum and hadronic-model directional pulls, driven by the `gs_interaction_model`/`gs_primary` response-model overrides on `MCEq3DFlux` (both `None` by default, i.e. inert). `diag_conservation.py` (Section 3.6) carries the first-principles derivation that Eq. (8) is exact straight-line 3D transport, the flat-atmosphere gate, and the decomposition behind the NA61 cone-width systematic; `test_conservation.py` is its unit-test companion, and the constraint options it exercises (`joint_cone.liouville_renorm`, `sky_scan`, `arrival_ray(r_earth=...)`, `ray=`) are default-off and proven to cancel identically in every zenith ratio. `diag_sk_ew.py` (Section 4.7) computes the flux-level confrontation with the Super-Kamiokande East–West asymmetry.
