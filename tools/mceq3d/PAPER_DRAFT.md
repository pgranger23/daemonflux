## Abstract

We extend the muon-calibrated, one-dimensional `daemonflux` model into a fully directional, absolute, all-flavour atmospheric-lepton flux $\Phi(E,\cos\theta_z,\varphi)$ over $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$, the range that drives atmospheric-neutrino oscillation measurements. The flux is a deterministic product of a per-zenith curved-atmosphere cascade, a first-principles off-axis 3D-production factor $E_{\rm off}$, and a cascade-correct full-IGRF geomagnetic cutoff. $E_{\rm off}$ — built from MCEq depth-resolved production, curved geometry, and the neutrino-versus-primary angle taken directly from the SIBYLL/UrQMD generator kernel folded with exact $\pi\to\mu\nu$ decay, with no hand-set hadronic parameters and no reference-flux input — produces the large sub-GeV near-horizon excess (horizon/vertical $\approx 1.8$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$) and is flavour-independent. With a Global-Spline-Fit-anchored hybrid base the model reproduces the Honda HKKM2014 solar-minimum tables to $\pm 11\%$ or better for both $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e$ at every zenith over $0.14$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The directional observables track the Honda and Bartol calculations, mostly within their mutual spread: the $\nu_e/\nu_\mu$ ratio to $\le 4\%$, the $\sec\theta$ horizon enhancement to $\approx 3$–$6\%$, the zenith shape (both flavours) to $\approx 5$–$15\%$ — the delivered cone uses the exact NA61-validated moment $\sigma_\pi$ after we root-caused a $\approx 16$–$37\%$ over-wide sampled generator kernel (a $\theta$-binning artefact), bringing $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e$ horizon/vertical onto *both* references to $\approx 0$–$8\%$ over $0.3$–$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Section 4.5) — and — using a channel-weighted, full-sphere production-cone-averaged geomagnetic cutoff with muon bending — the East–West asymmetry to $\approx 5$–$13\%$ at moderate near-horizon zenith (validated out-of-sample across zenith bands; a residual overshoot of $\approx 15$–$24\%$ remains at the extreme horizon, reduced from $\approx 25$–$29\%$ by extending the cutoff map continuously across the limb and folding in the muon-decay-channel cone width and in-flight bending). All systematics are delivered as named nuisance pulls on `daemonflux`'s calibration covariance: an NA61-driven $\pm 8\%$ hadronic term on the horizon excess, solar modulation, and the sub-GeV base-model spread ($\pm 14\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$) — a correlated error model the references lack. The cached geomagnetic cutoff makes the flux fast enough for routine oscillation fits.

## 1. Introduction

Atmospheric neutrinos are a cornerstone source for oscillation physics, from the original discovery of oscillations [1] to current and next-generation measurements of the mass ordering and mixing parameters at Super-Kamiokande, IceCube/DeepCore, Hyper-Kamiokande, DUNE, JUNO and KM3NeT/ORCA [1–5]. The interpretation of these data rests on a prediction of the atmospheric-neutrino flux as a function of energy and arrival direction. Below a few GeV — the region that drives the sub-dominant oscillation signatures — the flux prediction must be genuinely three-dimensional and must include geomagnetic effects, and the residual flux uncertainty in this region is among the leading systematics of the measurements.

State-of-the-art flux calculations (Honda *et al.* / HKKM [6,23], Bartol [7], FLUKA [8]) are full three-dimensional Monte-Carlo cascade simulations through the geomagnetic field and a realistic atmosphere. They are authoritative but computationally heavy, are tabulated for a fixed set of sites, and carry the hadronic-model uncertainties of their underlying interaction codes. A complementary approach, `daemonflux` [10], parametrises MCEq [11] cascade calculations with splines and calibrates them against a global set of muon measurements, yielding a fast, data-anchored flux *with a rigorous nuisance-parameter covariance*. By construction, however, `daemonflux` is one-dimensional: the neutrino is taken collinear with the primary, and the flux is azimuthally symmetric and free of any geomagnetic cutoff.

This work bridges the two: it retains the calibrated normalisation and uncertainty model of the 1D approach while adding the directional and geomagnetic structure of the 3D calculations, in a fast, deterministic engine that can be evaluated at an arbitrary site. We deliberately separate the physics into a factorised, trusted-ingredient construction (Section 3), validate each ingredient against data or against the authoritative 3D tables (Section 4), and quantify the irreducible low-energy uncertainty as an explicit systematic (Section 5). Section 6 discusses scope and limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2. The one-dimensional baseline

The starting point is the inclusive 1D flux $\Phi^{1D}_s(E,\theta_z)$ for each neutrino species $s \in \{\nu_\mu,\bar\nu_\mu,\nu_e,\bar\nu_e\}$. We support two choices. The first is a direct MCEq [11] solution with the SIBYLL-2.3d hadronic interaction model [12] and the Hillas–Gaisser H3a primary-flux parametrisation [14], solved per zenith in the realistic CORSIKA US-Standard layered atmosphere. The second, and our recommended choice for the absolute scale, is the muon-calibrated `daemonflux` flux [10]: it provides the $E^3$-weighted species sums and the $\nu/\bar\nu$ ratios, from which we reconstruct the four species (Section 3.2). The MCEq base is fully self-contained but carries the SIBYLL/H3a normalisation, which we find lies $\approx 25$–$30\%$ below Honda in the sub-GeV–few-GeV band (Section 4.1); the `daemonflux` base removes this deficit by anchoring to muon data.

**Outputs, conventions and uncertainties.** The engine returns the absolute differential flux $\Phi_s$ for the four species in $\mathrm{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}\,GeV^{-1}}$ on a logarithmic energy grid ($\approx 10$ bins per decade) spanning $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$, evaluable at arbitrary $(E,\cos\theta_z,\varphi)$ by trilinear interpolation (log-$E$, azimuth-periodic, $\cos\theta_z$). The same 1D base also carries the inclusive **muon** flux and charge ratio (charge-separated $\mu^\pm$), so a directional muon flux follows by the identical construction; we do not pursue it here. On uncertainties, two scopes are distinguished: (i) the **model/normalisation** spread between the two bases, delivered as an explicit systematic (Section 5); and (ii) daemonflux's own **nuisance-parameter (muon-calibration) covariance**, its principal feature, which **is propagated** to the directional flux (`solve(with_calib_error=True)`, Section 5). Because $G$ and $S$ are independent of the daemonflux parameters, the fractional calibration error is preserved exactly, $\sigma(\Phi_{3D})/\Phi_{3D}=\sigma(\Phi_{\rm df})/\Phi_{\rm df}$ (verified to $<0.1\%$).

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Deterministic-3D construction

The absolute directional flux is built from the per-zenith curved-atmosphere cascade, redistributed in arrival direction by the production angle and modulated by the geomagnetic cutoff,

$$
\Phi_s(E,\cos\theta_z,\varphi) \;=\; \Phi^{1D}_s\!\left(E,\,|\cos\theta_z|\right)\; E_{\rm off}\!\left(E,\cos\theta_z\right)\; G_s\!\left(E,\,R_c(\cos\theta_z,\varphi)\right),
\tag{1}
$$

where $\Phi^{1D}_s$ is the per-zenith 1D cascade (carrying the curved-atmosphere sec θ, Section 2), $E_{\rm off}=\Phi_{3D}/\Phi_{1D}$ is the first-principles off-axis 3D-production factor (Section 3.6) — the complete genuine-3D/1D production ratio that both redistributes flux in zenith and produces the sub-GeV near-horizon excess — and $G_s \in [0,1]$ is the cascade-correct response to removing primaries below the directional rigidity cutoff $R_c$. Each factor is a *first-principles or validated* piece: $\Phi^{1D}$ is muon-calibrated, $E_{\rm off}$ is built from MCEq production + curved geometry + decay kinematics (Section 3.6), $G$ is the first-principles rigidity response. The construction is genuinely 3D — every mechanism (curved geometry, off-axis production, geomagnetic anisotropy) enters — while remaining a fast deterministic product rather than a Monte-Carlo. The absolute value $|\cos\theta_z|$ enters the base because atmospheric production is up/down symmetric; the up/down asymmetry of the *observed* flux arises through $R_c$ (Section 3.5). $E_{\rm off}\to 1$ and $G\to 1$ at high energy, recovering the calibrated 1D flux. (Setting $E_{\rm off}=1$ gives the fast factorised path; it lies $\approx 1.8\times$ below the full construction near the sub-GeV horizon, Section 3.6.)

### 3.2 Species reconstruction from the data-anchored base

`daemonflux` reports the $E^3$-weighted flux *sums* and the species ratios. Writing $\Sigma_\mu \equiv E^3(\Phi_{\nu_\mu}+\Phi_{\bar\nu_\mu})$ and $r_\mu \equiv \Phi_{\nu_\mu}/\Phi_{\bar\nu_\mu}$ (and analogously for $e$), the individual species follow from

$$
\Phi_{\nu_\mu} = \frac{\Sigma_\mu}{E^3}\,\frac{r_\mu}{1+r_\mu},\qquad
\Phi_{\bar\nu_\mu} = \frac{\Sigma_\mu}{E^3}\,\frac{1}{1+r_\mu},
\tag{2}
$$

with the cm$^{-2}\to$m$^{-2}$ unit conversion applied. The reconstruction is valid over the calibrated range; below $\sim 0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the splines extrapolate beyond the muon data, an effect we treat as a systematic (Section 5).

### 3.3 Geomagnetic cutoff by trajectory back-tracing

The directional rigidity cutoff $R_c(\cos\theta_z,\varphi)$ is computed from first principles rather than from the analytic Størmer formula [17]. A cosmic ray of rigidity $R = pc/Ze$ moving with unit tangent $\mathbf{u}=d\mathbf{r}/ds$ obeys

$$
\frac{d\mathbf{u}}{ds} = \frac{c}{R}\,\big(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})\big),
\tag{3}
$$

i.e. a gyration of radius $r_g = R/(cB_\perp)$. To test whether a positive primary of rigidity $R$ can reach the detector from a direction $\mathbf{d}$, we integrate the *reversed* trajectory (reversed velocity and charge) outward from the detector with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta scheme in arc length: if it escapes to large radius the rigidity is **allowed**; if it returns to the Earth it is **forbidden** [16]. Scanning $R$ from high to low locates the effective cutoff at the allowed/forbidden transition. The field $\mathbf{B}$ is the full IGRF-13 model [15] (degree 13) near the Earth, smoothly matched to the tilted-dipole term beyond a few Earth radii where the higher multipoles are negligible.

As a closure test, replacing the field by a centred, aligned dipole reproduces the Størmer cutoff: $14.9\,\mathrm{GV}$ vertical at the geomagnetic equator, the $\cos^4(\lambda)$ latitude dependence, and the correct sign of the East–West asymmetry (positive primaries arrive preferentially from the West). For the full IGRF field at Kamioka we obtain a vertical cutoff of $11.31\,\mathrm{GV}$, consistent with the literature value ($\approx 11.3\,\mathrm{GV}$). Figure 1 shows the resulting cutoff sky map.

Because back-tracing each direction is expensive, the full $(\theta_z,\varphi)$ cutoff map is precomputed once per site and cached; subsequent evaluations are bilinear interpolations, making the first-principles cutoff fast enough for routine use. The analytic Størmer cutoff remains available as a dependency-free fallback.

### 3.4 Cascade-correct suppression and per-nucleus rigidity

The geomagnetic cut acts on the primary nucleons inside the developing shower, not on the neutrino energy. We therefore define the suppression factor as the ratio of two full cascade solutions,

$$
G_s(E,R_c) \;=\; \frac{\Phi_s\big[\text{primaries cut at } R_c\big]}{\Phi_s\big[\text{all primaries}\big]},
\tag{4}
$$

which automatically folds in the spread of primary energies feeding a given neutrino energy and avoids the common approximation of applying an effective-rigidity factor directly to the secondary spectrum.

The cut is on rigidity, $R = (A/Z)\,E_{\rm nucleon}$, so free protons ($A/Z=1$) and nucleons bound in heavier nuclei ($A/Z\simeq 2$) are removed at different nucleon energies. We split the primary nucleon flux accordingly, using MCEq's own proton and neutron spectra: the free-proton fraction is the proton–neutron excess,

$$
f_{\rm free}(E) = \frac{\Phi_p(E)-\Phi_n(E)}{\Phi_p(E)},
\tag{5}
$$

and the bound nucleons carry a composition-weighted $\langle A/Z\rangle$ from the H3a abundances ($\approx 2.005$, rising slowly with energy as the iron fraction grows). The per-nucleon transmission combines the two channels,

$$
T(E;R_c) = f_{\rm free}\,T_1(E;R_c) + (1-f_{\rm free})\,T_{\langle A/Z\rangle}(E;R_c),
\tag{6}
$$

with a smooth rigidity step (penumbra width $\sigma$ in $\ln R$),

$$
T_a(E;R_c) = \tfrac12\left[1 + \operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{\ln(aE)-\ln R_c}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)\right],\qquad a \equiv A/Z .
\tag{7}
$$

Neglecting the free/bound split over-suppresses the bound component near the sub-GeV horizon.

A potential concern is that $G_s$ is precomputed on the vertical column, whereas at large zenith the slant atmosphere is much thicker and the shower develops differently (more complete meson decay, more muon energy loss). We tested this directly by recomputing Eq. (4) in MCEq from vertical to $\cos\theta_z = 0.1$ ($\approx 84^\circ$): $G_s$ is zenith-independent to $\le 2.1\%$ at the worst point ($0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $R_c=11\,\mathrm{GV}$) and to $\le 0.4\%$ above $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Figure 2). The slant-depth effects act on numerator and denominator of Eq. (4) alike and largely cancel. An option to recompute $G_s$ per zenith is provided for studies that wish to remove even this residual.

### 3.5 The up-going hemisphere

For up-going directions the neutrino is produced on the far side of the Earth and traverses the planet essentially undeflected, so atmospheric production is identical to the down-going case at $|\cos\theta_z|$ (hence the absolute value in Eq. 1), but the geomagnetic cutoff must be evaluated at the **far-side production point**, not at the detector. Given the detector position and arrival direction we compute the line-of-sight intersection with the production shell at altitude $h\simeq 20\,\mathrm{km}$ on the opposite limb, and evaluate $R_c$ there with the primary direction set equal to the (straight-line) neutrino direction. This makes the up-going flux a genuine global-geomagnetic prediction. The leading approximation is the use of a single representative production point per direction; the finite extent of the production region is a sub-leading geometric correction.

### 3.6 The off-axis 3D-production factor $E_{\rm off}$

The genuinely-3D effect that a per-zenith cascade cannot produce is the *off-axis production geometry*: a neutrino arriving from zenith $\theta_z$ was created at a point $P$ at altitude $h$ along the arrival ray, by a shower whose axis (the primary direction) need not be the arrival direction — it lies within the production cone of the parent chain. In a *curved* atmosphere the local zenith of a direction steepens with altitude along a near-horizontal ray, so the slant depth $X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi)$ traversed by the parent — and hence the local neutrino production per slant depth $p(X,E)=d\Phi_\nu/dX$ — varies strongly across that cone near the horizon. Averaging the production over the cone therefore differs from the 1D (collinear) value:

$$
E_{\rm off}(\theta_z,E) = \frac{\displaystyle\int d\ell\,\rho(h)\,\big\langle\, p\!\left(X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi_p),E\right)\big\rangle_{\rm cone}}{\displaystyle\int d\ell\,\rho(h)\; p\!\left(X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi_o),E\right)},
\tag{8}
$$

with $\psi_o$ the arrival-direction local zenith at $P$ and $\psi_p$ the primary local zenith for a cone offset. Near the horizon ($\psi_o\!\to\!90^\circ$) the cone reaches primaries that arrived much more vertically — shallower $X_{\rm slant}$, younger shower, more sub-GeV production — while its opposite half points below the local horizon (blocked, $p\!\to\!0$); the asymmetry enhances the horizon at the expense of the vertical. $E_{\rm off}$ is therefore *predominantly a redistribution*, as an isotropic primary flux demands: its solid-angle average at fixed energy stays within $\approx 4$–$6\%$ of unity sub-GeV and $\to 1$ above a few GeV (at $0.2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ it peaks at $+77\%$ on the horizon and dips to $-9\%$ at the vertical), an independent, no-reference consistency check we verify explicitly (`diag_eoff_conservation.py`, `test_offaxis_conservation.py`). The small residual net enhancement ($\lesssim 6\%$ at $0.2$–$0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$) is consistent with the genuine sub-GeV total-flux enhancement seen in full 3D calculations. Two ingredients are taken directly from the cascade model: $p(X,E)$ from an MCEq depth-resolved cascade (SIBYLL-2.3d/H3a), and $X_{\rm slant}(h,\psi)$ from the curved-atmosphere CORSIKA density.

The cone width is the **pion production angle** $\sigma_\pi(E)$, which — consistent with the MCEq/daemonflux philosophy — we take from the *same generator* that provides the yields rather than parametrise: the inclusive $\langle\theta^2\rangle(E_{\rm meson})$ measured from the SIBYLL-2.3d / UrQMD-3.4 moments (regenerated through `chromo`, pooled by `fokker_planck_3d.load_theta2`, and validated against NA61/SHINE), folded with exact $\pi\to\mu\nu$ decay kinematics and weighted by the H3a primary spectrum (`kinematic_kernel.py`). There are **no hand-set transverse-momentum or Feynman-$x$ parameters**.

$E_{\rm off}$ is **flavour-independent**. The excess is a *production-rate* effect — set by where the parent pions are produced off-axis — so every daughter neutrino inherits it regardless of the decay chain ($\pi\to\mu\nu$ directly, or $\pi\to\mu\to e\nu\nu$). The near-isotropic muon-decay neutrinos therefore carry the *same* excess as the direct ones (they are **not** a flat pedestal); the neutrino's own decay-opening angle does not enter the cone, since it relocates individual neutrinos but not the pion-production excess. Kaons (a wider-angle, subdominant parent) are not folded in. One table is applied to each flavour's own 1D base; the much larger $\nu_e$ flux horizon-enhancement is entirely in the more horizon-peaked, muon-decay-origin $\nu_e$ base (Section 4.5). $E_{\rm off}\to 1$ at high energy and at the vertical. Setting $E_{\rm off}=1$ (`solve(offaxis=False)`) recovers the fast factorised path; the legacy flux-conserving redistribution (`solve(full_3d=True)`) is superseded by and must not be combined with $E_{\rm off}$. The data-driven hadronic uncertainty (NA61 $\pm12\%$ on $\sigma_\pi$) is $\pm8\%$ on the excess at the $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ horizon, falling to $\sim\!0$ by a few GeV, and is delivered as a nuisance pull (below); a multi-generator spread (EPOS/QGSJET) is the complementary systematic.

**Consistency with the muon calibration.** The **full** factor $E_{\rm off}$ is applied on every base, including the muon-calibrated `daemonflux` one, justified by an explicit closure check built into the table generation. The same off-axis machinery evaluated with the **muon** angular kernel (the generator pion angle folded with exact $\pi\to\mu\nu$ kinematics for the massive daughter) is consistent with unity in daemonflux's muon-calibration region (vertical directions, $E_\mu\gtrsim 5\,\mathrm{GeV}$: $E_{\rm off}^\mu = 0.998$–$1.000$). daemonflux's *neutrino* flux is itself genuinely 1D, and because $E_{\rm off}^\mu\simeq 1$ where the muon data live, the calibrated hadronic yields carry no hidden 3D normalisation for the full $E_{\rm off}^\nu$ to double-count; applying it therefore leaves the muon–neutrino calibration intact. Empirically the full factor also tracks Honda better than dividing out the vertical value (the sub-GeV vertical flux lands at $\approx 1.0\times$ Honda with the full factor versus $\approx 1.07\times$ if the vertical is normalised away). A still-cleaner design would inject the calibration one level deeper — at the MCEq hadronic yields rather than the 1D flux output — so muons and neutrinos share one calibrated cascade; this is discussed in Section 6.

### 3.7 Muon bending

Muons produced in the cascade bend in the geomagnetic field before they decay, so the decay neutrinos inherit a deflected direction. Over the in-flight decay the bending angle is

$$
\Delta\phi = \omega_c\,t_{\rm lab} = \frac{qB}{\gamma m_\mu}\cdot \gamma\tau_\mu = \frac{qB\,\tau_\mu}{m_\mu},
\tag{9}
$$

i.e. **energy-independent** (the longer flight of an energetic muon is exactly offset by its larger gyroradius), and numerically $\approx 3^\circ$ for the local field. What depends on energy is whether the muon decays in flight at all, governed by the decay-in-flight fraction

$$
f_{\rm dec}(E_\mu,\theta_z) = 1 - \exp\!\left(-\,\frac{L(\theta_z)}{\gamma\beta c\tau_\mu}\right),
\tag{10}
$$

with $L(\theta_z)$ the curved-atmosphere slant path from the production altitude. We weight the bending by $f_{\rm dec}/(1+f_{\rm dec})$ and restrict it to the muon-decay $\nu_\mu$ channel so it does not contaminate the direct $\nu_\mu$. The coherent, charge-dependent part is computed with the full local field vector (IGRF-13) and the actual muon direction: $\mu^+$ and $\mu^-$ deflect oppositely, giving a $\approx 3^\circ$ charge-separated East–West split and a small ($\approx 0.3^\circ$) net shift for the charge-*summed* flux given the near-unity muon charge ratio (Figure 4).

This coherent shift is **folded into the directional flux** through the channel-weighted geomagnetic cone (Section 3.4): the muon-decay component of each species' cone is centred not on the neutrino direction but on the **bending-shifted primary direction**, with the sign set by the parent muon charge ($\nu_\mu,\bar\nu_e$ from $\mu^-$; $\nu_e,\bar\nu_\mu$ from $\mu^+$). Because the four species are tracked separately (not charge-summed), each carries the *full* $\approx 3^\circ$ shift rather than the cancelled net — a genuinely charge-dependent directional effect relevant to $\nu/\bar\nu$ separation. Near the horizon, where the rigidity cutoff varies rapidly with direction, this shift moves the sampled cutoff and slightly reduces the residual E–W overshoot for both flavours (Section 4.1).

## 4. Validation

### 4.1 Absolute and directional flux versus Honda HKKM2014

We compare the absolute $\nu_\mu$ flux at Kamioka against the Honda HKKM2014 tables [6] on a dense energy grid from $0.1$ to $100\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Both predictions are log–log interpolated to common energies; a naïve nearest-grid-point comparison mismatches MCEq's $0.89\,\mathrm{GeV}$ node against Honda's $1.0\,\mathrm{GeV}$ bin and is misleading on a steep spectrum.

The comparison (Table 1, Figure 5) reveals the role of the 1D base. The raw-MCEq base lies $\approx 25$–$30\%$ below Honda across $0.3$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$, a genuine SIBYLL/H3a normalisation deficit. The `daemonflux`-anchored base agrees with Honda to $\approx 10\%$ for $E\gtrsim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ up to $100\,\mathrm{GeV}$, but over-predicts toward $0.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (up to $\sim\!2\times$), where it extrapolates beyond its muon calibration. The two bases **bracket Honda below $\sim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}$** (daemonflux above, MCEq below); above $\sim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ both lie below Honda, with the data-anchored daemonflux base the closer of the two.

The sub-GeV discrepancy of the raw-MCEq base is traced to the **primary spectrum, not the hadronic model**: the H3a parametrisation under-predicts the measured nucleon flux in the $\sim\!5$–$30\,\mathrm{GeV}$ parent region (by $\approx 32\%$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$) relative to the data-driven **Global Spline Fit** (GSF, fitted to AMS-02/BESS/PAMELA — precisely the dedicated low-energy dataset the sub-GeV flux needs). Re-running the same SIBYLL-2.3d cascade with the GSF primary brings the MCEq base into $\approx\pm10\%$ of Honda at $0.15$–$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, while the muon-calibrated daemonflux base remains superior above $\sim\!1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — the two are best in *complementary* domains with a crossover near $0.8\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The recommended configuration is therefore the **hybrid base** (`base_model="hybrid"` with the GSF primary): GSF-anchored MCEq below, muon-calibrated daemonflux above, blended smoothly over one octave at $E_0=1.7\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (`hybrid_weight`) — the daemonflux muon-calibration floor ($E_\mu\ge 5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, per its documentation) mapped to neutrinos via $E_\nu\simeq E_\mu/3$, a physics-derived (not fitted) crossover; a scan over $E_0\in[0.5,1.7]\,\mathrm{GeV}$ moves the delivered ratios by $\le 6\%$ below $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and confirms the derived value. Two further quantified items: seasonal atmospheric variation (MSIS00, January/July) changes the vertical $\nu_\mu$ flux by $\lesssim 0.3\%$ over $0.3$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (negligible for neutrinos), and the force-field knob now supports **de-modulation** ($\varphi<0$: $S(-0.35\,\mathrm{GV})=1.13$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$), enabling exact epoch matching to the solar-minimum reference tables. With the full engine (geomagnetic cutoff and $E_{\rm off}$) this reproduces the Honda solar-minimum tables to **$\pm 11\%$ or better for both $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e$, at every zenith, over $0.14$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$** (e.g. vertical $\nu_e$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$: $1.11$, versus $1.64$ with the daemonflux base alone) — Honda/Bartol-competitive across the full range. The inter-base spread remains the model systematic (Section 5), now between two much closer, independently-anchored models. (In these comparisons the reference epoch matters: the Honda Kamioka table used here is empirically pinned to **solar minimum** — it matches Bartol's solar-min tables to $\lesssim 4\%$ below $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ versus $7$–$17\%$ for solar-max — while the GSF epoch is a data-averaged one, leaving a $\sim\!\pm10\%$ sub-$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ epoch ambiguity that the solar-potential pull covers.)

The directional observables are ratios and are therefore independent of the absolute base; all numbers here are from the **delivered engine** (Figure 6). The $\sec\theta$ horizon/vertical enhancement tracks Honda to $\approx 3$–$6\%$ across the whole range ($1.27$ vs $1.23$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$; $2.33$ vs $2.19$ at $100\,\mathrm{GeV}$; slightly high at multi-GeV, following the base). The $\nu_e/\nu_\mu$ flavour ratio — the sharpest, most model-independent test, set by the $\pi\to\mu\to e$ chain — agrees with Honda to $0.7$–$4\%$ across the band. The **East–West asymmetry** is where the extension matters most, and where we take care to compare like with like — an earlier version of this work compared this work at $75^\circ$ against Honda's $\approx\!87^\circ$ horizon bin, a zenith mismatch that flattered the agreement. Quoted at a **matched** near-horizon zenith ($\cos\theta_z=0.25$, $\approx\!75^\circ$, the same band for both), the production-cone-averaged cutoff has the correct sign, the sub-GeV peak, and the vanishing above $\sim\!10\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and tracks Honda's amplitude with a systematic **overshoot of $\approx 9$–$13\%$** across $0.4$–$2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ ($2.44$ vs $2.23$ at $0.6\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $2.06$ vs $1.85$ at $1.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $1.64$ vs $1.46$ at $2\,\mathrm{GeV}$; $+13\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, falling to $\lesssim\!+6\%$ by $5\,\mathrm{GeV}$) — within the Honda–Bartol mutual spread. This is a large improvement of the *shape and magnitude*: the pre-fix configuration (single detector cutoff, $20\,\mathrm{GV}$ rigidity cap) *under*-produced the amplitude by a comparable factor ($\approx14\%$ deficit at this zenith). The fix has four first-principles parts, none tuned to Honda. **(i) A rigidity-cap bug.** The cutoff back-trace scanned rigidities only up to $r_{\rm hi}=20\,\mathrm{GV}$; but the near-horizon *East* cutoff at Kamioka *exceeds* $20\,\mathrm{GV}$, so it was clamped, under-suppressing the East. Raising the ceiling to $40\,\mathrm{GV}$ (and extending the $G_s(R_c)$ grid to match) corrects the contrast — but a single sight-line cutoff is then maximally sharp and over-shoots. **(ii) Production-cone averaging.** The parent primary arrives from a *spread* of directions (the production cone, $\sim\!20^\circ$ sub-GeV), each with its own cutoff; averaging $G$ over that cone — the geomagnetic analogue of $E_{\rm off}$, weighted by the same $\sigma_\theta(E)$ — smears the sharp single cutoff down toward Honda (the $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $75^\circ$ amplitude falls from $3.08$ to $2.6$). We separately **ruled out** the analytic $\mathrm{erf}$ penumbra width (sharpening it changed the amplitude by $<10\%$ and could not close the deficit) and the hadronic cone-width systematic (propagating the NA61 $\pm12\%$ pion-angle pull through the cone via `cone_sigma_scale` moves the amplitude by only $\pm2\%$), so the residual overshoot is neither. **(iii) A channel-weighted cone.** The single cone above used the *pion* production angle for every neutrino, but $\approx40\%$ of sub-GeV $\nu_\mu$ (and $\approx98\%$ of $\nu_e$) are born from **muon decay**, whose primary-to-neutrino angle is wider — the generator pion angle folded with the muon and Michel decay kinematics, plus an *energy-independent* in-flight-bending floor ($\approx4.8^\circ$). We average $G$ over both cone widths and blend per species by the MCEq muon-decay flux fraction $f_\mu$ (Section 3.7): the wider muon-decay cone smears the muon-decay component more, and the bending floor supplies smearing at multi-GeV where the pion cone has vanished. **(iv) Muon bending.** The muon-decay cone is centred on the charge-signed **bending-shifted** primary direction (Section 3.7), which near the horizon moves the sampled cutoff. Together (iii)–(iv) lower the $75^\circ$ overshoot by a further $\approx2$–$4$ points (e.g. $+20\%\to+13\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$) and — as they are largest for the muon-decay-dominated flux — bring $\nu_e$ closer as well; they are first-principles refinements (MCEq fractions, generator angle, IGRF bending), not tuned to Honda. A residual overshoot remains, dominated by the intrinsically sharp geomagnetic contrast that a factorised $G\times\Phi_{1D}$ (as opposed to a fully coupled 3D transport) cannot fully soften.

**Out-of-sample check.** Because both parts were introduced while addressing this discrepancy, we validate them at zenith bands *not used in the diagnosis* (which was at $75^\circ$). Scanning Honda's own $\cos\theta_z$ bins, the matched E–W amplitude agrees to $\approx 5$–$6\%$ at $63^\circ$ and $\approx 8$–$11\%$ at $69^\circ$, with the overshoot growing toward the extreme horizon. Its growth there had a specific, first-principles cause: near the horizon the $\sim\!20^\circ$ production cone extends *below the local horizon*, and in an earlier version of this work the down-going-only cutoff map was clamped at the limb, so the cone under-smeared exactly where the geomagnetic contrast is sharpest. We remove that clamp by building the cutoff map over the **full sphere** — the down-going detector cutoff stitched continuously onto the up-going far-side cutoff (Sections 3.3, 3.5) — so the sub-horizon part of the cone samples the *real* cutoff below the limb (the two hemispheres join smoothly, e.g. $40.0\to39.0\,\mathrm{GV}$ East and $7.8\to7.8\,\mathrm{GV}$ West across $89^\circ\!\to\!90^\circ$ at Kamioka). This is not a tuning knob: the up-going cutoff is the same validated global back-trace already used for up-going neutrinos. The limb-continuous map reduces the extreme-horizon overshoot from $\approx 25$–$29\%$ to $\approx 20$–$25\%$ at $87^\circ$ and from $\approx 18$–$21\%$ to $\approx 18\%$ at $81^\circ$ — largest where the cone crosses the limb most — while leaving the moderate-zenith bins and the $75^\circ$ diagnosis band unchanged. The channel-weighted cone and muon-bending shift (parts iii–iv above) then bring it further, to $\approx 15$–$24\%$ at $87^\circ$ ($0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$: $+29\%\!\to\!+15\%$ across all four fixes), $\approx 13$–$16\%$ at $81^\circ$, $\approx 6$–$9\%$ at $69^\circ$ and $\approx 4$–$6\%$ at $63^\circ$. A residual overshoot remains at the extreme horizon, dominated by the intrinsically sharp geomagnetic contrast that a factorised $G\times\Phi_{1D}$ cannot fully soften; we verified it is *not* a numerical artefact (the production-cone quadrature is converged to $<1\%$ on doubling), *not* the finite production-region displacement (evaluating $R_c$ at the true near-horizon production point, hundreds of km away, changes the East cutoff by only $\approx6\%$), and *not* the Honda azimuth binning ($\lesssim1\%$). It is a known boundary of the factorised treatment, not a free parameter; closing it fully requires a coupled 3D transport (Section 6). The natural *data-level* out-of-sample test is a direct comparison against the Super-Kamiokande **measured** East–West asymmetry [26] — the first observation of the effect in atmospheric neutrinos — which requires folding the detector response and is left to an analysis-level study. The tables and normalisation in this subsection are quoted at (near-)vertical, where $E_{\rm off}$ is close to unity (it reduces the vertical $\nu_\mu$ by only $\approx 7\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\lesssim 2\%$ above $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$); its principal effect is on the zenith *shape* (Section 4.5).

### 4.2 Hadronic production versus NA61/SHINE

MCEq's *stored* interaction matrices are $p_T$-integrated ($\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}x_L$, no angular information), so we regenerate the **two-dimensional** production kernels $\mathrm{d}^2N/(\mathrm{d}x_L\,\mathrm{d}p_T)$ by re-running the same generators (SIBYLL-2.3d, UrQMD-3.4) through `chromo` in single-interaction mode and histogramming in $(x_L,\theta)$ (`kernel_regeneration.py`); it is this 2D kernel that the off-axis factor samples directly (Section 3.6). Marginalising it over $p_T$ reproduces MCEq's stored $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}x_L$ bin-for-bin (the pipeline's consistency gate). We validate the *angular* content of the 2D kernel against fixed-target data: the mean transverse momentum $\langle p_T\rangle(p)$ of charged pions and kaons in $p+\mathrm{C}$ at $31\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$ against the NA61/SHINE measurements [18,19] (HEPData [22] records ins886780 and ins1397003, fetched via `hepdata-cli`), applying the experiment's angular acceptance. For pions the agreement is $\approx 10\%$ (Figure 7); for kaons UrQMD reproduces the $\langle p_T\rangle$ scale ($0.2$–$0.6\,\mathrm{GeV}$) and its rise with momentum, running $10$–$20\%$ harder than the data (Figure 8), and enters $E_{\rm off}$ only through the subdominant kaon term. This $\pm 12\%$ $\langle p_T\rangle$ uncertainty is propagated as the `sigma_pi_NA61` nuisance pull (Section 5).

### 4.5 The near-horizon 3D excess, from first principles

The largest genuinely-3D feature is the **sub-GeV near-horizon enhancement**: at the horizon ($\cos\theta_z\!\approx\!0.05$) the full-3D references give horizon/vertical $\approx 1.8$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $1.5$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $1.25$–$1.3$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, whereas a per-zenith cascade alone gives $\approx 0.9$–$1.05$ there. It is *robust* — Honda and Bartol, two independent 3D Monte-Carlos, agree on it to $\approx 5$–$15\%$ — and it is exactly what the off-axis production factor $E_{\rm off}$ of Section 3.6 produces. We stress that $E_{\rm off}$ is built with **no input from any reference flux**, so both Honda and Bartol here are genuine, independent tests of the derivation.

Folding in $E_{\rm off}$, the delivered zenith shape $\Phi(\cos\theta_z)/\Phi(\mathrm{vertical})$ reproduces both references across the full range (Figure 11). The horizon/vertical ratio, ours versus Honda [Bartol], is $1.83$ vs $1.89$ [$1.76$] at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $1.54$ vs $1.52$ [$1.43$] at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $1.38$ vs $1.25$ [$1.30$] at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and $1.68$ vs $1.62$ [$1.73$] at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (where the excess is the ordinary $\sec\theta$ rise carried by the base). At $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and above $\sim\!3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the delivered shape sits *within* the mutual Honda–Bartol spread. An earlier construction drove the pion cone with the sampled full $(x_L,\theta)$ generator kernel (`pion_alpha_pdf`, `k_spliced.npz`) and **overshot both references** at $0.5$–$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — by $\approx 12\%$ ($\nu_\mu$) and $\approx 5$–$6\%$ ($\nu_e$) at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, beyond the $\approx 4$–$6\%$ Honda–Bartol mutual spread. We root-caused this: the sampled kernel's per-secondary **meson-angle RMS is $\approx 16$–$37\%$ wider** than the exact moment $\sigma_\pi$ (`m_spliced.npz`), the discrepancy growing with energy as the true angle shrinks toward the kernel's coarse $0.667^\circ$ $\theta$-bins — a histogram bin-centre inflation of the forward-peaked production angle (`diag_kernel_consistency.py`). The moment $\sigma_\pi$ is computed *exactly* ($\theta=\arctan(p_T/p_L)$ per secondary, no binning) and is NA61-$\langle p_T\rangle$-validated, so it is the accurate input. **The delivered cone therefore uses the moment $\sigma_\pi$** (a Gaussian of the validated variance; `offaxis_mc.py` default, legacy sampled kernel via `--cone-kernel sampled`), which brings both flavours onto the references: $\nu_\mu$ horizon/vertical vs Honda is $1.00$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $1.08$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (was $1.07$, $1.14$), $\nu_e$ $0.96$ and $1.02$ (was $\approx1.05$, $1.06$), with $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ preserved (`diag_cone_fix.py`). The residual $\nu_\mu$ overshoot ($\approx 7$–$8\%$ at $1$–$3\,\mathrm{GeV}$) is then the MCEq base sec$\,\theta$ running slightly high, not $E_{\rm off}$ (`diag_isolate.py`: the base alone exceeds Honda above $\approx 2\,\mathrm{GeV}$). One boundary: below $\approx 0.15\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — the extrapolation edge, where cones reach $\approx 40^\circ$ and daemonflux's base extends beyond its muon calibration — the flux-conservation residual of $E_{\rm off}$ grows to $\approx 8\%$ at $0.11\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (`test_offaxis_conservation.py`), a documented limit of the factorised geometry rather than a modelled prediction. The high-energy limit is recovered exactly ($E_{\rm off}\!\to\!1$). This nonetheless turns the near-horizon excess from what was, in an earlier version of this work, a reference-anchored correction into a genuine first-principles prediction that reproduces the effect and its energy dependence for both flavours, with the mid-sub-GeV magnitude accurate to $\approx 10\%$.

The *same* $E_{\rm off}$ reproduces the $\nu_e$ zenith shape equally well (base$\times E_{\rm off}$ versus Honda [Bartol] at the $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ horizon: $2.08$ vs $2.18$ [$2.01$]; $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$: $1.81$ vs $1.76$ [$1.84$]) — an agreement of $\approx 5\%$ sub-GeV, the same as $\nu_\mu$. This is because **$E_{\rm off}$ is flavour-independent**: the excess is a geometric property of *where* the parent mesons are produced in the curved atmosphere, inherited by every daughter neutrino regardless of the $\pi/K$/muon decay chain. Indeed the excess implied by the references themselves ($\mathrm{ref\text{-}shape}/\mathrm{base\text{-}shape}$) agrees between $\nu_e$ and $\nu_\mu$ to $\approx 5\%$ at every energy; the much larger $\nu_e$ *flux* horizon-enhancement (horizon/vertical $\approx 2.2$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, rising to $\approx 4.6$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$) arises entirely from the more horizon-peaked 1D $\nu_e$ base (muon-decay origin), which the engine applies per flavour. The residual on $E_{\rm off}$, comparable to the inter-reference spread, is dominated by the production-angle kernel model; a full continuous off-axis transport is the natural refinement.

## 5. Systematic uncertainties

It is important to separate two distinct sources here, which earlier drafts of this work conflated. The **interaction-model** uncertainty — the genuine ambiguity in the hadronic yields at fixed primary flux — is *small*: running SIBYLL-2.3d, EPOS-LHC, DPMJET-III and QGSJET-II through the identical setup (`hadronic_spread.py`) gives a spread of only $\approx 4$–$7\%$ sub-GeV (rising to $\approx 12$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV). Consistently, the two independent *full-3D* references agree far better than the historical folklore "$20$–$40\%$": Honda and Bartol track each other to $\approx 4$–$15\%$ across $0.1$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Section 4.4). The larger uncertainty that dominates *our* sub-GeV budget is instead the **overall-normalisation / primary-flux / calibration** ambiguity — historically the reason different groups' absolute fluxes scattered — which we quantify from the spread of our two independently data-anchored bases *around the recommended central*. The recommended central is the **GSF-anchored hybrid** $\Phi_{\rm hyb}$ (Section 4.1); the band is its half log-spread against the muon-calibrated daemonflux base $\Phi_{\rm df}$,

$$
\sigma_{\rm sys}(E) = \tfrac12\left|\ln\!\frac{\Phi_{\rm df}}{\Phi_{\rm hyb}}\right| ,\qquad
\Phi_{\rm central} \equiv \Phi_{\rm hyb} .
\tag{11}
$$

Recomputed around this recommended central, the band is $\pm 20\%$ at $0.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\pm 15\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\pm 14\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\pm 5\%$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and collapses to $\lesssim 1\%$ above the $\sim 2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ crossover (Figure 12) — roughly **half** the earlier raw-H3a-MCEq$\leftrightarrow$daemonflux band ($\pm 31\%$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$), which had inflated the sub-GeV uncertainty with the H3a-primary base ($\approx 25$–$30\%$ low over $0.3$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$) that we recommend against. This is the number to propagate through an oscillation fit as a correlated normalisation-versus-energy systematic. Two points make it coherent and complete. First, above the crossover $\Phi_{\rm hyb}\equiv\Phi_{\rm df}$, so this base-spread term vanishes *by construction*; the residual uncertainty there is carried by the two other pulls — the muon-calibration covariance ($\approx 3$–$5\%$) and the hadronic interaction-model spread ($\approx 10$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV, below) — which the delivered covariance folds in quadrature, so the total model uncertainty is never understated. Second, being a spread of two data-anchored models it is an *intra-framework* systematic; it does not by itself span other calculations, but Honda and Bartol (which agree to $\approx 4\%$, §4.4) both sit within it sub-GeV, and the direct Bartol comparison (§4.4) plus the hadronic-model spread (below) supply the inter-calculation ingredients. Sub-dominant, well-bounded effects include the production-angle treatment ($\sim 1$–$2\%$), the zenith-independence of $G_s$ ($\le 2\%$, Section 3.4), the composition-averaged bound $\langle A/Z\rangle$, and the muon-bending kinematic constants, which matter only for fine-grained charge-resolved studies.

![**Figure 12.** The two data-anchored 1D bases versus Honda, with the recommended GSF-hybrid central and the model-spread band (GSF-hybrid $\leftrightarrow$ daemonflux) over $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The raw H3a-MCEq curve (faint) is shown for reference; the recomputed band around the hybrid central is roughly half the old H3a$\leftrightarrow$daemonflux envelope and collapses above the $\sim 2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ crossover.](base_comparison.png)

**Decomposing the spread: interaction model versus calibration.** To separate the hadronic-interaction contribution from the overall normalisation, we run the same base setup (H3a primary, US-Standard atmosphere) through the four hadronic models MCEq carries — SIBYLL-2.3d, EPOS-LHC, DPMJET-III-19.3, QGSJET-II-04 — a proxy for the inter-calculation (Bartol/FLUKA) spread whose dominant driver is the hadronic model. The **interaction-model spread is only $\approx 4$–$7\%$ sub-GeV, rising to $\approx 12$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV** (Figure 13). This is much smaller than the daemonflux$\leftrightarrow$MCEq spread below a few GeV, which establishes an important point: the **sub-GeV flux uncertainty is dominated by the overall normalisation/muon-calibration, not by the interaction model** — precisely why anchoring to muon data matters. At multi-GeV the two contributions become comparable ($\sim 10\%$ each). We caution that this MCEq-internal spread is *not* a substitute for a literal inter-code comparison — those codes differ also in primary flux, atmosphere and 3D treatment — but it bounds the hadronic-model part; the direct Bartol comparison (§4.4), which found Honda and Bartol consistent to $\approx 4\%$, supplies the complementary inter-code check, and a FLUKA table can be ingested through the same path when available.

![**Figure 13.** Hadronic interaction-model spread of the vertical $\nu_\mu$ base (four MCEq models, same primary/atmosphere): $E^3\Phi$ (left) and ratio to the geometric mean with the inter-model envelope (right). The spread is $\approx 4$–$7\%$ sub-GeV, rising to $\approx 12$–$13\%$ at multi-GeV — smaller than the calibration-driven spread of Figure 12 below a few GeV.](hadronic_spread.png)

**Solar modulation.** A distinct, *time-dependent* effect is the 11-year solar cycle, which modulates the sub-GeV primary flux. We include it as an optional Gleeson–Axford force-field knob on the primary (`solar_factor`, `solve(solar_modulation=φ)`): the potential $\phi$ [GV] is applied per nucleon with the rigidity-dependent energy loss ($Z/A\,\phi$; free protons lose the full $\phi$, bound nucleons half), and the resulting neutrino-energy factor $S(E)$ — obtained through the cascade, hence species- and $\sim$zenith-independent like $G_s$ — rescales the flux. Taking $\phi\approx 0.4\,\mathrm{GV}$ (solar minimum) and $\approx 1.0\,\mathrm{GV}$ (solar maximum), the predicted solar-cycle amplitude $\Phi(\mathrm{min})/\Phi(\mathrm{max})$ is $+22\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $+14\%$ at $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $+2\%$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (Figure 14), consistent with the $\sim 10$–$20\%$ sub-GeV variation reported by Honda and Bartol. The potential is defined relative to the H3a baseline ($\phi=0$); the physical observable is the difference between two potentials, so a time-integrated analysis should either fix $\phi$ to the data-taking epoch or carry the min–max band as a systematic.

![**Figure 14.** Solar-modulation factor $S(E)$ for three force-field potentials (left), and the solar-cycle amplitude $\Phi(\mathrm{min})/\Phi(\mathrm{max})$ versus energy (right): $\approx +22\%$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ falling to $\approx +2\%$ at $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$, matching the sub-GeV solar-cycle variation of the full 3D calculations.](solar_modulation.png)

**Propagated muon-calibration uncertainty.** The model-spread band above is a *between-model* systematic; distinct from it is daemonflux's *within-model* nuisance-parameter covariance — the calibrated error budget that is the 1D model's defining strength. Because $\Phi_{3D}=\Phi_{\rm df}\,G\,S$ with $G$ and $S$ independent of the daemonflux parameters, both the 1-$\sigma$ error and the full parameter covariance carry through by scaling each daemonflux gradient by $G\,S$; in particular the fractional error is unchanged. We propagate it with `solve(with_calib_error=True)`, returning the absolute $1\sigma$ (`flux_err`) and fractional (`flux_relerr`) calibration uncertainty per species, with `calib_hadronic_only` isolating the hadronic-production component. At Kamioka the propagated calibration uncertainty on $\nu_\mu$ is $\approx 3$–$5\%$ over $0.3$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (hadronic-dominated), and the preservation $\sigma(\Phi_{3D})/\Phi_{3D}=\sigma(\Phi_{\rm df})/\Phi_{\rm df}$ is verified numerically to $<0.1\%$ (Figure 15). For a fit that needs the *correlated* nuisance structure rather than the diagonal band, `with_calib_jacobian=True` returns the 24 daemonflux nuisance-parameter names, their correlation matrix, and the fractional per-parameter Jacobian $R_i=\partial\ln\Phi/\partial\eta_i$ (identical for base and directional flux); `calib_covariance` then assembles the full energy–energy covariance $\mathrm{Cov}=(\Phi R)^\top\!\rho\,(\Phi R)$ for any direction. This reproduces daemonflux's `error()` to machine precision ($<10^{-11}$) and yields the expected correlations (e.g. $\rho(1,3\,\mathrm{GeV})=0.99$, $\rho(1,30\,\mathrm{GeV})=0.67$), so an oscillation fit can carry daemonflux's pulls as correlated nuisance parameters directly on the 3D flux. This calibration band ($\sim$few percent) and the model-spread band (Figure 12, larger sub-GeV) are complementary and should both enter an analysis; the former is the appropriate within-daemonflux systematic, the latter the base-choice/extrapolation uncertainty.

**One covariance for everything.** The remaining systematics are likewise delivered as unit-variance nuisance pulls appended to the same parameter set, so a single call returns the *complete* error model: `with_eoff_jacobian=True` adds the hadronic off-axis pull `sigma_pi_NA61` (the NA61 $\pm12\%$ pion-angle response, $\approx\pm8\%$ on the sub-GeV horizon excess, $\to 0$ by a few GeV); `solar_sigma_gv` adds a solar-potential pull of the chosen $1\sigma$; and `with_base_spread=True` adds the fully-correlated base-model-choice pull (the half log-spread between the MCEq and daemonflux bases — the dominant sub-GeV systematic of Section 5). These pulls are mutually uncorrelated and uncorrelated with the 24 calibration parameters (block-diagonal correlation); `calib_covariance` then assembles the full energy–energy covariance including all of them, and `flux_relerr`/`flux_err` fold them in quadrature. An oscillation fit therefore carries calibration, hadronic-production-angle, solar and model-choice uncertainties as named, correlated nuisance parameters on the directional flux — the daemonflux error philosophy extended to every 3D ingredient.

## 6. Discussion and limitations

We are explicit about the trust boundary. The delivered engine (Section 3, Eq. 1) is the complete deterministic-3D construction — curved per-zenith cascade $\times$ first-principles off-axis production $E_{\rm off}$ $\times$ geomagnetic cutoff $\times$ solar — validated absolutely against Honda and Bartol and at the kinematic level against NA61. The large genuinely-3D **sub-GeV near-horizon excess** (horizon/vertical $\approx 1.8$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, Section 4.5) is now produced *from first principles* by $E_{\rm off}$ (Section 3.6) — MCEq depth-resolved production, curved geometry, and the pion production angle taken from the SIBYLL/UrQMD generator moments (NA61-validated) folded with exact decay, with no hand-set hadronic parameters and no reference-flux input — reproducing Honda and Bartol to within their mutual $\approx 5$–$15\%$ across $0.3$–$10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ for **both** $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e$ (Section 4.5). $E_{\rm off}$ is flavour-independent — a single production-geometry factor applied to each flavour's own 1D base — which the $\nu_e$ comparison confirms. This supersedes the earlier reference-anchored treatment of this term. The uncertainty on $E_{\rm off}$ is data-driven: the NA61 $\pm12\%$ on the pion production angle propagates to $\pm8\%$ on the sub-GeV horizon excess (falling to $\sim\!0$ by a few GeV); the complementary multi-generator (EPOS/QGSJET) spread requires regenerating the moments with those generators. The cone now uses the **sampled** angular distribution from the generator's full $(x_L,\theta)$ pion kernel folded with exact decay (Gaussian fallback only where kernel statistics are thin, $E\gtrsim$ few GeV where the cone is small), and the **kaon parent** enters as a separate term with its own MCEq depth profile and $\sigma_K$ — the two former approximations are now computed.

We note that $E_{\rm off}$ **is** the exact straight-line 3D neutrino transport, not a factorisation of it: because neutrinos do not scatter, the full coupled transport from production to detector is the altitude-resolved cone average of Eq. (8), which is precisely what the engine evaluates (the production cone is averaged *at every altitude* along the arrival ray, `offaxis_mc`). An independent from-scratch ray-traced re-implementation (`coupled_3d_transport.py`, a self-contained curved-atmosphere cascade + Eq. 8) reproduces the delivered $E_{\rm off}$ to a few percent away from the extreme horizon (diverging near the limb only through its simplified airmass model), and confirms that the altitude resolution is *necessary* — a naive single-altitude factorisation of the same integral errs by up to $\approx 50\%$ near the horizon. Consequently there is no hidden neutrino-*streaming* residual: the only genuinely-3D terms $E_{\rm off}$ omits are **charged-shower lateral development** (secondaries spreading before neutrino production, coupling adjacent columns) and — like the geomagnetic cross-coupling of Section 4.1 — the coupling between the directional cutoff and the shower development. Both are bounded small ($\sim 1$–$2\%$ on the conventional flux for the former; $\lesssim 15\%$ on the extreme-horizon E–W for the latter).

Crucially, closing them does **not** require abandoning the fast-deterministic approach for a Monte-Carlo: they call for a fully-coupled 3D cascade, but such a cascade can be built *deterministically* — a "3D MCEq" in which the state is (species $\times$ energy $\times$ angular moments) marched in depth, with a sparse angular-transport operator (validated to machine precision in `spherical_streaming`) and a **geomagnetic Lorentz-force operator** on the charged species. The cutoff itself remains the global back-trace (optimal by Liouville); the deterministic cascade adds only the local force and the inter-direction coupling. A de-risking spike (`geomag3d_spike.py`) validates the two prerequisites: the force operator (a full-sphere rotation of the direction distribution) is exact, and although the sharp near-horizon cutoff Gibbs-rings under a raw $P_N$ truncation ($\sim 11\%$, non-convergent), the physically-required production-cone low-pass tames it to $<0.1\%$ at a feasible degree $\ell_{\max}\!\approx\!16$ (or $S_N$ discrete ordinates carry it sharply). A first assembled version of the solver (`mceq3d_deterministic.py`) marches the coupled (species $\times$ energy $\times$ direction) cascade on the full sphere with the production-cone coupling and the Lorentz-force operator: it reduces *exactly* to the per-direction 1D cascade when the couplings are switched off, and the East–West asymmetry emerges self-consistently from a cutoff-structured primary source (with the in-cascade force a small rider, as expected — the coherent bending lives in the muon-decay channel). The parametrised cascade has since been replaced by **MCEq's real interaction and decay matrices** (`mceq3d_real.py`): marching $\Phi\!\mathrel{+}=(\mathsf{int\_m}\,\Phi + \mathsf{dec\_m}\,(\rho^{-1}\Phi))\,\mathrm{d}X$ ourselves for the full direction set reproduces `MCEqRun`'s own solution to machine precision (max rel diff $0$), so the directional flux now carries the **absolute, calibrated SIBYLL/H3a physics** while still exposing the between-step hooks for the 3D couplings. The **curved per-direction columns** are in place (`march_curved`): each arrival direction develops down its own slant atmosphere (MCEq's per-zenith density profile), so the $\sec\theta$ horizon enhancement is carried self-consistently and reproduces MCEq's per-zenith solution to machine precision (a naive common-grid step blows up near the horizon, where the slant grammage exceeds the forward-Euler stability limit and demands MCEq's fine adaptive stepping). The inter-direction couplings are therefore applied by **operator-splitting at common altitude checkpoints** (`march_checkpoints`): each column is marched with MCEq's stable fine steps between checkpoints, and the geomagnetic force is applied across directions *at* each checkpoint (the charged-species blocks rotated by the per-direction path length accumulated over the segment). With the couplings off this reproduces the per-zenith cascade *exactly* (the correctness anchor); with them on, the directions couple self-consistently during the real cascade. The **production-cone inter-direction spread** is likewise in place: because neutrinos do not propagate after production, the neutrinos *created in each segment* are exactly the flux increment, and are spread over the production cone (width $\sigma_\theta(E)$, the same generator angle as $E_{\rm off}$), flux-conservingly, coupling adjacent directions. On a fine whole-sky grid (validated in `validate_3d_deterministic.py`) this spread reproduces the high-energy $\sec\theta$ enhancement (carried by the curved columns: horizon/vertical $1.36$ vs Honda $1.29$ at $3\,\mathrm{GeV}$) but sub-GeV it nets to angular *smearing* rather than $E_{\rm off}$'s off-axis *excess* — the emergent factor $\Phi_{\rm cone}/\Phi_{\rm no\,cone}\approx 0.97$ ($<1$), versus the delivered $E_{\rm off}=1.2$–$1.35$ at $0.3$–$0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ near the horizon. This is a genuine, informative result: **spreading the accumulated arrival-direction neutrino flux is not the same as the off-axis excess**, which is a *production-vertex* effect — the cone reaches primaries that traversed a *shallower slant depth* (younger shower, higher sub-GeV production per depth), a term that spreading arrival flux does not inject. Capturing it deterministically requires applying the cone *at the production vertex* — sourcing each arrival direction's neutrinos from the cone of primary columns evaluated at *their* slant depth — not by spreading the accumulated arrival flux. We have now done this and it **closes the loop**: `mceq3d_prodvertex.py` takes the local production profile $p(X,E)=\mathrm{d}\Phi_\nu/\mathrm{d}X$ from an *independent* hand-march of MCEq's real matrices (`mceq3d_real.march_profile`, not the `offaxis_mc` solve), integrates it through the curved-atmosphere production-vertex cone, and reproduces the delivered $E_{\rm off}$ to **$\approx 1$–$2\%$** across $0.3$–$3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (horizon/vertical $1.61$ vs $1.59$ at $0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $1.30$ vs $1.29$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$), with two gates passing exactly: the factor $\to 1$ as the cone width $\to 0$ ($\max|E_{\rm off}-1|=5\times10^{-4}$, the collinear reduction) and $\to 1$ at high energy. This confirms $E_{\rm off}$ is not an artefact of its own construction but the genuine production-vertex result of the real cascade, and it explains why the earlier arrival-flux and parent-spreading couplings could not reproduce it — for a free-streaming neutrino there is *no* transport coupling; the entire 3D effect is the production geometry that $E_{\rm off}$ already integrates (the sole remaining transport-like piece, charged-parent bending, is the separate, small muon-decay term of Section 3.7). The closure here uses the vertical (universal-in-$X$) production profile and shares the curved-geometry slant-depth table and cone kernel with $E_{\rm off}$; the only untested refinement is the *per-direction* curved-density profile (the zenith-dependent decay competition $\rho(X)$), an expected $\approx 1$–$2\%$ correction — not a missing mechanism. With the neutrino excess closed, we tested whether the analogous *charged*-sector coupling closes the residual extreme-horizon East–West overshoot (`coupled_ew_diag.py`): the real-IGRF cutoff-modulated primary marched with curved columns, the production-cone neutrino smear, and the in-cascade Lorentz force on the mesons/muons. The result is instructive and negative: the production-cone smear reproduces the delivered `cone_geff` level ($W/E\approx 3.0/2.6/2.0$ at $0.5/1/2\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $87^\circ$, vs the delivered $2.9/2.5/1.9$) and the in-cascade meson/muon bending is a genuine $<1\%$ rider, so the coupled charged march **still overshoots Honda by $\approx 20$–$30\%$** — the same as the factorised model. (One caveat surfaced: the Lorentz force must be applied *only* to cascade-produced mesons/muons; bending the nucleons in-cascade double-counts the primary deflection already contained in the back-traced cutoff and spuriously collapses $W/E$ to $\approx 1.3$.) The extreme-horizon overshoot is therefore **not** a missing inter-direction coupling. We then localised it further (`diag_ew_cause.py`, `diag_ew_cause2.py`): evaluating the cutoff at the true displaced production point (only $\approx 272\,\mathrm{km}$/$3^\circ$ away at $87^\circ$, $h\!\sim\!20\,\mathrm{km}$) lowers the East cutoff by just $40\!\to\!38.5\,\mathrm{GV}$ and softens $W/E$ by only $\approx 4$–$5$ points ($+37\%\!\to\!+32\%$ single-cutoff), so the production-point displacement is a $\lesssim 5\%$ effect, not the cause. With the correct back-traced cutoffs and the cascade-correct $G_s$ (which cancels between East and West up to the primary-energy mapping), the residual overshoot sits in the $G_s(R_c^W)/G_s(R_c^E)$ ratio and **grows with energy** ($+15\%\!\to\!+24\%$ over $0.5\!\to\!2\,\mathrm{GeV}$) — a signature the (energy-flat) penumbra width cannot produce, pointing away from the penumbra. We then tested the natural suspect — the hadronic interaction model — directly: recomputing $G_s$ with **DPMJET-III-19.3** (Honda's generator family, native to the MCEq database; `diag_ew_dpmjet.py`) at fixed primary and cutoffs removes only $\approx 4$–$6$ points ($\sim\!1/6$) of the overshoot, so the SIBYLL-vs-DPMJET difference is **not** the cause either. Having ruled out inter-direction coupling ($<1\%$), the production-point displacement ($\lesssim 5\%$), and the interaction model ($\sim\!1/6$; DPMJET-III-19.3 leaves the overshoot essentially intact), we localised the cause by inverting both our and Honda's $W/E$, with our cascade-correct $G_s$ and a common West cutoff, to the *effective* East cutoff each implies (`diag_ew_invert.py`). The result is clean: Honda's implied East cutoff is $\approx 27$–$29\,\mathrm{GV}$ and **energy-flat**, whereas ours is higher and **rises with energy** ($\approx 33\to 39\,\mathrm{GV}$ over $0.5\to 2\,\mathrm{GeV}$). Our *raw* back-traced cutoff ($\approx 42\,\mathrm{GV}$ East) is not at fault — it is Störmer-consistent and our vertical value ($11.3\,\mathrm{GV}$) matches the literature; the real back-traced transition is moreover a near-sharp step ($\Delta\ln R\!\approx\!0.01$; `diag_ew_penumbra.py`), so a too-soft penumbra is excluded — our `erf` is if anything already softer than reality. The discrepancy is therefore in the *softening of that sharp cutoff*: our factorised production-cone average (`cone_geff`) softens the $42\,\mathrm{GV}$ step down to $\approx 33\,\mathrm{GV}$-effective at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ but only $\approx 39\,\mathrm{GV}$ at $2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ — because the cone half-width $\sigma_\theta(E)$ narrows with energy — while Honda's full-3D Monte-Carlo, integrating the extended near-horizontal production region and the trajectory ensemble, softens it further ($\approx 28\,\mathrm{GV}$) and *uniformly* in energy. **This is the factorisation limit, now quantified**: a first-order cone average of the sharp geomagnetic cutoff under-softens the extreme-horizon East–West relative to a full 3D cascade, and does so energy-dependently, which is exactly the growing-with-energy overshoot. It is consistent with the coupled-march test above (a deterministic cone smear reproduces `cone_geff`, not Honda). We tested the obvious candidate for the extra, energy-flat softening — averaging the cutoff over the extended near-horizontal **production region** rather than at the detector (`diag_ew_prodregion.py`): production-weighting $R_c$ along the line of sight lowers the effective East cutoff by only $\approx 2\,\mathrm{GV}$ ($40\to 37.7$), because the sub-GeV production peaks only $\approx 200$–$400\,\mathrm{km}$ out where $R_c$ is still $\approx 38\,\mathrm{GV}$. So cutoff *averaging* — angular (`cone_geff`, $\to 33$–$39$) or spatial (production region, $\to 38$) — does **not** reach Honda's effective $\approx 28\,\mathrm{GV}$. The residual softening is angular: decomposing the delivered $W/E$ (`diag_ew_muon.py`) shows it is very sensitive to the production-cone width (a $\times 1.5$/$\times 2$ widening moves $W/E$ from $2.65$ to $2.34$/$2.09$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$), confirming that the missing ingredient is angular smearing of the sharp cutoff. But the first-principles pieces we already carry provide little of it: the channel-weighted muon-decay cone and `muon_bending` together soften $W/E$ by only $\approx 4\%$ ($2.77\!\to\!2.65$). The conclusion is that the extreme-horizon overshoot is a **multi-cause systematic with no single fixable knob**: (i) the factorised angular softening of the sharp cutoff is too weak — in particular the energy-flat muon-decay/bending floor ($\approx 4.8^\circ$) is too small to soften the *high-energy* East–West, where the pion cone $\sigma_\theta(E)$ has narrowed, so the overshoot grows with energy and a *uniform* cone widening (which would in any case be tuning) cannot close it ($\times 2$ still leaves $+10\%$ at $2\,\mathrm{GeV}$); (ii) the primary model contributes $\approx 8\%$ (GSF gives $W/E=2.87$ vs H3a $2.65$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$); and (iii) the interaction model $\approx 1/6$. None is a coupling, penumbra, raw-cutoff, or cutoff-averaging effect. We therefore quote the extreme-horizon East–West as an irreducible systematic of the fast factorised treatment ($\lesssim 15$–$24\%$ at $87^\circ$, within the Honda–Bartol model spread at the zeniths that dominate oscillation analyses), and do not tune it to Honda. The solver is also fast enough for the fine direction grids that the physics validation needs: the integration path is obtained without a full solve, and the independent per-direction marches run in parallel across cores (scipy's sparse matrix–vector product releases the GIL), so a whole-sky grid is a matter of minutes rather than hours, at identical (machine-precision) per-direction physics.

**On the factorised architecture and where the calibration is injected.** The construction is deliberately factorised — $\Phi_{1D}\times E_{\rm off}\times G\times S$ — for speed, per-factor validatability, and to inherit `daemonflux`'s calibration and covariance; the full-3D references instead solve one coupled Monte-Carlo. Two distinct approximations follow. The first, the deterministic $E_{\rm off}/G$ surrogates for native 3D transport, is validated to $\approx 5\%$ above. The second is subtler and worth stating plainly: we inject the muon calibration at the level of `daemonflux`'s **1D flux output** and multiply corrections onto it, whereas the calibration physically lives in geometry-agnostic **hadronic yields** (the Barr-style $\pi/K$ production parameters and the GSF primary). The cleaner architecture would apply those calibrated yields to the MCEq cascade directly, so that muons and neutrinos are produced by a single calibrated 3D cascade and the muon–neutrino consistency is automatic rather than checked *post hoc*. The GSF primary is already injected at this level (the hybrid base, Section 4.1); the hadronic (Barr) part is not, because the public `daemonflux` package ships flux-gradient splines and the parameter covariance but not the parameter$\to$yield recipe (block definitions and baseline model) needed to reproduce it inside an arbitrary cascade — recovering it is a project best done with the calibration's build code. In its absence, the muon closure of Section 3.6 ($E_{\rm off}^\mu\simeq 1$ in the calibration region) is what guarantees the flux-level factorisation does not corrupt the calibration; the yield-level refit is the natural route to a genuinely unified (rather than corrected-1D) directly-calibrated 3D model.

**The East–West asymmetry, previously the leading directional limitation, is now largely reproduced — with an honest, understood residual.** Earlier drafts reported the delivered E–W as $\approx 20\%$ too weak at $0.5$–$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (against a mismatched horizon bin). Rather than tune it, we diagnosed it and found two first-principles causes (Section 4.1). First, a **rigidity-cap bug**: the cutoff back-trace scanned only to $r_{\rm hi}=20\,\mathrm{GV}$, but the near-horizon East cutoff at Kamioka exceeds that, so it was clamped and the East under-suppressed (the fixed-width analytic penumbra, the obvious first suspect, was tested and **ruled out**). Raising the ceiling to $40\,\mathrm{GV}$ restores the contrast, but a single sight-line cutoff is then maximally sharp and over-shoots. Second, the physically-required smearing: the parent primary arrives from a *spread* of directions (the production cone), each with its own cutoff — a genuinely-3D coupling the factorised $G\times\Phi_{1D}$ drops. **Averaging $G$ over the production cone** (weighted by the same $\sigma_\theta(E)$ that drives $E_{\rm off}$) smears the sharp single cutoff toward Honda. Both are implemented and on by default (`cone_cutoff=True`, built on a cached IGRF cutoff map via `cutoff_map`/`finemap_rc`; one-time cost $\sim\!6.5\mathrm{k}$ trajectories per site/date). At a *matched* near-horizon zenith the amplitude now has the right sign, peak and high-energy behaviour and sits $\approx 6$–$14\%$ from Honda (Section 4.1) — a large improvement over the prior deficit, at the edge of the Honda–Bartol spread — with a systematic overshoot that grows toward the extreme horizon (limitation v below). We regard the E–W as demonstrated in shape and magnitude across the sky, with a well-localised, understood residual, rather than claim exact agreement.

Other known approximations, each bounded above: (i) the up-going far-side production point — verified insensitive to production altitude ($\le 0.5\%$ on $R_c$ over $15$–$30\,\mathrm{km}$; its finite angular extent is the separate $E_{\rm off}$ effect); (ii) the high-statistics SIBYLL kernels degrade near the generator's soft-pion centre-of-mass threshold; (iii) meson re-interactions are neglected (sub-GeV-negligible); and (iv) the muon-bending kinematic constants ($E_\mu\approx 3E_\nu$, the muon charge ratio) are fixed rather than derived. One approximation is specific to the production-cone cutoff of Section 4.1: **(v)** the cone of a *down-going* neutrino now extends continuously across the limb — the cutoff map is built over the **full sphere**, the down-going detector cutoff stitched onto the up-going far-side cutoff, so the sub-horizon part of the cone samples the real cutoff below the local horizon rather than clamping at $\theta_z=89^\circ$ (Section 4.1). This reduced the extreme-horizon E–W overshoot from $\approx25$–$29\%$ to $\approx20$–$25\%$ at $87^\circ$; the channel-weighted cone and muon-bending shift (below) then bring it to $\approx15$–$24\%$. The remaining overshoot is the intrinsically sharp geomagnetic contrast a factorised treatment cannot fully soften — we verified it is *not* the cone quadrature (converged to $<1\%$ on doubling), *not* the finite production-region displacement ($\approx6\%$ on the East cutoff), and *not* Honda's azimuth binning ($\lesssim1\%$). One residual refinement remains: the *up-going* neutrino directions themselves still take the single far-side cutoff (their own cone extension is the direct far-side analogue). **(vi)** the geomagnetic cone weight is now **channel-weighted**: the wider muon-decay cone — the muon-and-Michel-decay angle plus an energy-independent in-flight-bending floor — is blended with the direct pion cone by the MCEq muon-decay flux fraction, and the muon-decay cone is centred on the charge-signed **bending-shifted** primary direction (Section 3.7). The sub-dominant **kaon** parent (wider $\sigma_K$) is carried in $E_{\rm off}$ but not yet in the E–W cone, a $\lesssim2\%$ sub-GeV effect (Section 4.1). $G_s$ is now recomputed per zenith by default (`zenith_dependent_geomag=True`), so the former $\le 2\%$ vertical-column approximation is removed from the delivered flux. The largest single uncertainty remains the physical sub-GeV model spread of Section 5.

**Performance.** On a single core, one MCEq cascade solve takes $\approx 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$. A full directional solve over a $6\times 8 = 48$-direction sky grid takes $\approx 6\,\mathrm{min}$ — roughly $240\times$ one MCEq solve, or $\approx 14\times$ the equivalent 1D MCEq flux at the same zeniths. The cost is dominated ($\sim 85\%$) by the geomagnetic-cutoff trajectory back-tracing ($\sim 5\,\mathrm{s}$ per direction); the 1D base contributes $\approx 26\,\mathrm{s}$ (MCEq base) or $\approx 10\,\mathrm{ms}$ (daemonflux spline base), and the suppression response $G_s$ adds $\approx 21\,\mathrm{s}$. Both heavy ingredients are reusable and are memoised to disk (`solve(use_cache=True)`): the cutoff map is a one-time per-site precompute and $G_s$ is site- and zenith-independent, so a warm re-evaluation loads from disk in milliseconds (measured: a warm solve is $\approx 5\,\mathrm{ms}$ versus $\approx 60\,\mathrm{s}$ cold, a $>10^3$ speed-up, reproducing the direct result to $\approx 0.1\%$). The framework is therefore a one-time per-site precompute followed by near-instant evaluation, in contrast to the CPU-weeks of a full 3D Monte-Carlo.

The daemonflux **nuisance-parameter covariance** is now fully propagated to the directional flux (Sections 2 and 5): both the $1\sigma$ calibration band and the *full correlated covariance* (the 24 pulls, their correlation matrix, and the per-parameter Jacobian) are exported, reproducing daemonflux's `error()` to machine precision, so a global fit can carry daemonflux's calibrated, correlated nuisance parameters directly on the 3D flux. One capability remains deliberately out of scope: the **correlated muon** accompanying a down-going neutrino — the atmospheric self-veto passing fraction relevant to neutrino telescopes — is an event-level quantity that an inclusive flux model (this work, MCEq, or daemonflux) cannot provide; it requires a dedicated calculation such as nuVeto [25].

The seasonal and site dependence of the atmosphere is available through the NRLMSISE-00 profiles exposed by MCEq, but has not been studied here. The former high-value extension — anchoring the sub-$0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ region to a dedicated low-energy dataset — is now realised through the GSF-primary hybrid base (Section 4.1), which ties the sub-GeV central value to the AMS-02/BESS/PAMELA primary data rather than to an extrapolation.

## 7. Conclusions

We have presented a fast, deterministic, data-anchored framework that extends the 1D `daemonflux` model into a fully directional, absolute, all-flavour atmospheric-neutrino flux over $0.1$–$100\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Each ingredient is either taken from a trusted source or validated against data: the absolute scale is anchored to muon-calibrated splines and agrees with Honda to $\approx 10\%$ above $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$; the geomagnetic cutoff is a first-principles full-IGRF back-trace, cached for speed; the directional observables match the authoritative Honda 3D tables; and the production kinematics match NA61/SHINE. The irreducible sub-GeV uncertainty is quantified as an explicit systematic band. The framework is suitable for oscillation analyses that require a directional flux at an arbitrary site with a transparent, propagatable error model, and provides a fast complement to full 3D Monte-Carlo calculations.

We particularly invite expert feedback on the choice and sub-GeV extrapolation of the 1D base; the cascade-correct definition of the geomagnetic suppression and the per-nucleus rigidity split; the far-side treatment of the up-going hemisphere; and whether the model-spread systematic is an appropriate way to present the irreducible low-energy uncertainty to an oscillation analysis.

## Appendix A: numerical methods

The cascade solutions use MCEq with its default 1D matrix solver on a logarithmic energy grid of $\approx 10$ bins per decade from $0.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The trajectory back-tracing (Eq. 3) integrates with a fixed-step RK4 in arc length; the escape radius and step length are chosen so that the recovered Størmer cutoff is stable to better than $1\%$. The cutoff sky map is precomputed on a $(\theta_z,\varphi)$ grid and cached as a compressed array; production evaluations bilinearly interpolate it. The systematic band (Eq. 11) is evaluated pointwise in energy from the two base predictions.

## Appendix B: code and data availability

All code, unit tests, and figure-generating scripts are available on the fork `github.com/pgranger23/daemonflux`, branch `3d-extension`, under `tools/mceq3d/`. The absolute engine is `mceq3d_flux.py`; the first-principles off-axis 3D-production factor is built by `offaxis_mc.py` with the generator pion production angle (SIBYLL/UrQMD moments via `chromo`, folded with exact decay) in `kinematic_kernel.py`; the geomagnetic back-tracer and cached cutoff are in `geomag_backtrace.py` (the fine cutoff sky map of Figure 1 is `make_cutoff_map_fig.py`); the validation scripts are `validate_honda.py`, `validate_ew_zenith.py` (the out-of-sample East–West zenith scan of Section 4.1), `validate_na61.py`, `validate_na61_kaon.py`, `validate_bartol.py`, `base_comparison.py`, `hadronic_spread.py`, `solar_modulation.py`, `calib_uncertainty.py`, `offaxis_mc.py --validate`, `verify_offaxis.py` (zenith/cutoff-independence and convergence of the off-axis factor), `geomag_zenith_check.py` and `latitude_check.py`; the off-axis modules carry dedicated unit tests (`test_offaxis_mc.py`, `test_kinematic_kernel.py`, including the muon-calibration closure); performance and caching are exercised by `profile_3d.py` and `verify_cache.py`. A detailed development report with the complete module inventory and an exhaustive list of approximations is provided in `IMPLEMENTATION_REPORT.md`.
